% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-08 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book III, batch 6: sheets 61 to 72 - twelve leaves that fall into two halves,
% and the join between them is the largest change of subject the volume has
% made since leaf 9.
%
% Nine things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The work book ends inside this batch. Leaves 79 to 89 are the last six oils,
% laid out as batches 4 and 5 laid them out: Edward's ink record drawing at the
% head, then the title and the size, then where and when it was painted, then
% the canvas and the colours, then her description, then the sale. Leaf 89 is
% The Two Commedians, finished 10 November 1966, and it is the last picture
% Edward Hopper painted; the leaf carries no sale and nothing follows it. Then
% the foliation jumps from 89 to 101, and from leaf 101 the book is a
% watercolour list: two works to a leaf, a drawing to each, a title, a size, a
% month, and one line of money. Budget attention accordingly - the oils are
% slow and carry three worked payment blocks between them, the watercolours are
% quick and carry twelve one-line sales.
%
% The gap at 91 to 99 is a gap in the Whitney's sequence, not a defect in the
% record: the foliation is the stationer's, printed in blue at the top of a
% recto, and the leaves between were not photographed. Nothing here says what
% is on them.
%
% The canvas is « Herga » on all six oils that name one, and the batch settles
% its spelling by unsettling it: leaves 79 and 81 write « Herga » in quotation
% marks, as leaves 69 to 77 did, and leaf 89 writes « Herger » plainly, which
% is a fifth spelling of the word after National, Herga, Winton and the
% retraced capital of leaf 57. « single prime » stands unstruck on every leaf
% that carries it, where four leaves of batch 5 struck it. Flake White
% throughout, except leaf 89, which says lead white.
%
% Three of the six oil leaves carry a worked money block rather than a line,
% and two of the three are the hardest passages in either half of the batch.
% Leaf 79 braces two payments on account against a price of 15 000 and adds an
% asterisked note that the second cheque was lost in the post. Leaf 85 is the
% densest block in the volume so far: a price, a gallery commission, two
% receipts a year apart, a discount, a final payment, a bank, and a run of
% later ink over pencil that will not resolve - three of the batch's five
% \ill{} are in it. Leaf 81 works its third to the cent and reconciles: 25 000
% - 8333.33 = 16 666.67, paid 6 000 and 10 666.67 seven months apart.
%
% The arithmetic reconciles everywhere except leaf 109, where it fails twice
% in the same terms. « 750 - 1/3 = 600 » is written for Construction in Mexico
% and again for Mount Moran, both plainly, both in ink, on one leaf; leaves 101
% and 103 take the same third correctly (600 - 1/3 = 400, 750 - 1/3 Com. = 500)
% and leaf 105 does it twice more. Nothing on leaf 109 says why. It is as
% written and has not been corrected.
%
% Two watercolours were not sold but exchanged, and the two leaves tell the
% same transaction from opposite ends. Leaf 107 says El Palacio is now owned by
% the Whitney, swapped for Lombard's House, an earlier purchase, when it came
% out of the March 1950 retrospective. Leaf 109 says the same of Construction
% in Mexico - taken by the Whitney out of the Hopper Retrospective in exchange
% for Lombard House, a watercolour of 1931 bought in February 1934 for 750,
% « Even Exchange. 750 - 750 » - and then strikes both lines through with a
% single thin stroke each and sells the picture to Chas. E. Buckley in blue ink
% above them. The two leaves cannot both be right about which picture went to
% the Whitney and neither has been reconciled with the other here.
%
% She writes in pencil, rubs it out and inks over it on both halves of the
% batch, and on four leaves the erased layer is still legible: leaf 83 (an
% erased size beside the title), leaf 87 (an erased size, two figures and an
% « x »), leaf 101 (an erased first draft of the Saltillo Mansion title line)
% and leaves 103 and 105, where the pencil under « Bought by Paul Mallon » adds
% « Washington D.C. » and the pencil under « Bought by Stephen Clark » says
% nothing the ink does not. Transcribe the ink; the erasures are in \note{}.
%
% Two spellings of one place on one leaf, which is the Book II habit arriving
% here: leaf 105 writes « Monterrey Cathedral » on its upper entry and
% « Sierra Madre at Montervey » on its lower, four lines apart, both legible.
% Leaf 101's head says the watercolours were done from the roof of the
% « Guarhado House » and its fore-edge note says « Casa Guajardo ». Both stand.
%
% The fore-edge of leaf 101 carries the only paint formula in the watercolour
% half, written vertically up the margin and covering all six Mexican sheets at
% once: Winsor Newton tube water colors on Whatman rough paper, four done from
% the roof of the Casa Guajardo at Saltillo and two from a hotel window at
% Monterrey. Leaf 109 has a second vertical marginal, « Peter Juley - 1943
% photo. », beside a leaf whose own head is 1946.
%
% Her titles are in quotation marks on two leaves (79, 81) and underlined on
% four (85, 87, 89, 109); the watercolour titles carry neither. Following
% batches 3 to 5, the marks and the underlines are not carried into \work{} -
% they are noted here rather than silently dropped. The year stands in the head
% margin of the five oil leaves that have one, in pencil throughout; leaves 87
% and 89 have no head at all. The watercolour leaves replace the year with a
% standing head naming the trip - « Watercolors - Mexico Summer 1943 »,
% « Water Colors, Mexico 1943 », « Watercolors Mexico 1943 », « Mexico - May +
% June 1946 » twice, and « 1946 » alone on the last.

\note{the two payments are braced together against the total, and the total is

in ink, and « unmuted » at the end of the frame line is in pencil too. The

same man as \uncertain{Huydenryk}. The title is in quotation marks on the
