% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-07 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book III, batch 2: sheets 13 to 24 - the last five Reviews and Reproductions
% leaves, the Gifts leaf, the One Man Shows leaf, and then the volume proper,
% which opens at leaf 1 with Hotel Lobby.
%
% Seven things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The batch turns a corner in the middle. Sheets 13 to 19 finish the standing
% lists that the front of Book III is made of, and carry no money at all: seven
% leaves of magazines, newspapers, catalogues, radio broadcasts, gifts of
% etchings and one-man shows, written in running prose with no ruling of her
% own. At sheet 20 the volume becomes a work book, and every leaf after it is
% one picture: Edward's ink record drawing, then the title and the size, then
% her paint formula, then her description, then the sale.
%
% The foliation is printed, in blue, at the head of the leaf, and it is
% odd-numbered: 1, 3, 5, 7 on the four work leaves here. The even leaves are
% the versos, and the Whitney photographed one of them - the leaf carrying the
% two notes about Morning in a City - as an unnumbered sheet even though a
% printed 4 stands at its head. The catalogue is what \sheet{} must agree with,
% so that sheet takes an empty second argument and the note on it says what is
% printed on the paper. The seven list leaves before them are unnumbered in
% fact as well as in the catalogue.
%
% Leaf 1 is not written on the ruled leaf. The record - drawing, title, paint,
% description - stands on a plain sheet of Croxley paper laid over the leaf,
% whose printed 1 shows above it; the blind stamp reads in mirror, so it is the
% back of that sheet we are looking at. Book V's leaves do the same thing and
% batch 1 there records it.
%
% She writes these work leaves in pencil first, rubs the pencil out, and inks
% over it. All four show the erased layer, and on leaf 1 the two layers do not
% agree: the erased line dates Hotel Lobby « Jan. 11 1943 » where the ink above
% it reads « Jan. 4", 1943 ». Both are on the leaf and both stand. Each paint
% formula is closed by a short ruled line with a small x on it, in her hand, on
% every one of the four.
%
% Her coloured underlining goes on doing the job batch 1 found it doing - it
% underlines the picture titles so a leaf of magazine references can be read
% down them - and it acquires two more colours: an orange crayon on sheets 14
% to 17, and a blue crayon used on a handful of titles on sheets 16 and 17. It
% is not a correction and does not survive into the transcription.
%
% Her braces are everywhere on these leaves, grouping two, three or four
% consecutive lines against one date or one exhibition written at the right.
% They are described in \note{} and never transcribed, because a brace asserts
% a relation between rows that a transcribed character would not carry.
%
% The four sales at the end reconcile, which is worth saying because they often
% do not: 2500 - 1/3 = 1666 2/3, 2200 - 1/3 = 1466 2/3, 6000 - 1/3 = 4000,
% 3500 - 1/3 = 2333 1/3. Nothing here has been totalled or checked into the
% transcription; the arithmetic is hers and it is sound.
