% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-08 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book III, batch 4: sheets 37 to 48 - twelve leaves of the work book, running
% from leaf 31 to leaf 53 on the odd numbers, with no written verso among them.
%
% Seven things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The batch is the most regular in the archive so far. Twelve leaves, twelve
% oils, one to a leaf, and every one of them laid out the same way: Edward's
% ink record drawing at the head, then the title and the size, then where and
% when it was painted and when it went to Rehn, then the canvas and the
% colours, then her description, then the sale. Batch 3 had a verso, a lawsuit
% running to eight lines and a leaf the Whitney left unnumbered. This one has
% none of that. Where the earlier batches record a book still deciding what it
% is, this one records a book that has decided.
%
% The foliation is the stationer's, printed in blue at the top of a recto, and
% runs 31, 33, 35, 37, 39, 41, 43, 45, 47, 49, 51, 53. The even leaves are
% versos and not one of them is written on, so not one of them is
% photographed. The gap in the Whitney's sequence is the whole record of it.
%
% Not a single ruled table in the batch, so not a single \begin{ledgertable}.
% This is worth saying because it is the fourth batch in a row of a volume
% whose first fifteen leaves are nothing but ruled lists: from leaf 9 the book
% stops ruling and never starts again, and the sale that would be four columns
% in Book I is one sentence here.
%
% The colourman is the constant. Eleven of the twelve leaves name Winsor \&
% Newton (National) double prime canvas and W \& N. colours; the twelfth, leaf
% 33, breaks the run with single prime British canvas and Blockx Silver White,
% and she says it is very thick. The white changes under her hand as the batch
% goes on - flake white to leaf 41, then Chremnitz White from leaf 43 to the
% end, with leaf 43 itself naming both. She is recording a change of materials
% in real time and nowhere remarks on it.
%
% The money is one line to a leaf and the arithmetic is hers throughout: 3000 -
% 1/3 = 2000, 1200 - 1/3 = 800, 3500 - 1/3 = 2333 1/3 four times, 4000 - 1/3
% com. = 2666.67, 4000 - 1/3 = 2666.66, 4050 - 1/3 = 2700, 4500 - 1/3 = 3000
% twice. Nothing has been totalled or checked into the transcription. Leaf 43
% is the exception that proves it a habit: Morning Sun is not one line but a
% worked column - selling price, commission, a receipt on account, a balance
% due and the day it was paid - and it is the only place in the batch where
% she sets the sum out rather than stating its answer.
%
% The buyers are named on every leaf, which is new. Book I records the dealer
% and rarely anybody else; here Rehn is a place the picture goes and the person
% who bought it is written down: Anthony Haswell, Lynn Farnol, Rockefeller,
% Hirshhorn three times over four years, Stephen Clark, Mrs. Otto Spaeth, the
% Metropolitan and Columbus museums. Three of the private buyers get an address
% underneath in pencil. Hirshhorn's surname is spelt Hirshhorn on leaves 39 and
% 45 and Hirshorn on leaf 51, and both stand.
%
% She writes in pencil, rubs it out and inks over it, exactly as batches 2 and
% 3 found. On leaves 35 and 53 the rubbed pencil is still legible under the ink
% and carries a different date from it, so both are given. On leaf 33 the
% pencil beside the title is rubbed past reading and is \ill{}.
%
% Her titles are in quotation marks on nine of the twelve leaves. Following
% batch 3, which set the convention for this volume, the marks are not carried
% into \work{} - they are hers and they are on the paper, and they are noted
% here rather than silently dropped.

\section{High Noon}

\sheet{16818}{31}

\sketch{}

\work{High Noon - \quad 28" x 40"}

\hand{jo}{Painted in Truro studio in October \quad To Rehn in November 1949\\
Winsor \& Newton (National) double prime canvas and W \& N. colors,\\
flake white, linseed oil and turpentine}

\hand{jo}{White clapboard house, gray shingle roof, deep gray shadows on eaves
+ roof.\\
Red chimney + foundation, burnt grass + upper window shades pale yellow ochre\\
Sky cerulean, horizontal shapes of mackerel sky white, green along horizon,
lower left.\\
Blond female in stringy blue kimono open in front over possibly naked body,\\
effect sloppy, but such a hot day!\\
Sold \uncertain{one} of Whitney Retrospective 1950.}

\hand{jo}{Anthony Haswell \qquad 3000 - 1/3 = 2000. Dec. 29, 51.}

\hand{jo}{E.H. made little model of house out of heavy white paper to observe
how light would fall on corners of house.}

\note{the buyer's name stands in the left margin, outside the ruled area and
level with the sale, which is how the leaf sets it; the sentence about the
paper model is below the sale and in a paler pencil, so it was added after it.
« burnt grass » and « mackerel sky » are both readings that the letters carry
plainly; « one of Whitney Retrospective » is the doubtful one, and « out of »
would fit the sense better than the strokes do.}

\hand{jo}{Painted in Truro studio in October 1950\\
Winsor \& Newton (National) double prime canvas and W \& N.\\
colors, flake white, linseed oil + turpentine}

\hand{jo}{Sept. fields not glowing except tufts of light across grass. Blue sky

Blondish housewife (appraising weather) in pink cotton dress. Interior

\note{the only sale in the batch at a price that is not a round hundred, and

offered is the first. The abbreviation before « Museum » is « incl. » or
« ind. » and the sense wants the first. « (thru Whitney) » is in pencil below
the ink line and is the only part of the sale she did not ink over.}

\hand{jo}{Jos. H. Hirshhorn - Sept. 30, 1954. \quad 3500 - 1/3 = 2333 1/3
\qquad this + 11 A.M. bought together for 5500.}

\marginal{Com. --- above the dash between « 1/3 » and « 2333 1/3 »}

\note{the longest description in the batch, and the only one that says what was
happening to Edward while he painted. « lacking indistinction » is as written
and is not a slip for « lacking distinction »: the sentence before it is about
how little the figures beyond the first row are allowed, and the phrase
continues that. Two or three characters at the end of the line before « virus »
are struck through and do not resolve. The last clause of the sale is the
useful one and it is not arithmetic: this canvas and 11 A.M. went to the same
buyer on the same day for 5500 between them, which does not reconcile with the
3500 written three inches to its left, and the leaf does not reconcile it
either.}

\section{Morning Sun}

\sheet{16521}{43}

\marginal{1952. --- in pencil in the head margin, above the ruled area}

\sketch{}

\work{Morning Sun \quad 28 x 40}

\hand{jo}{Painted in New York Studio in February 1952\\
Winsor \& Newton (National) double prime canvas\\
and W \& N Colors, Flake White, Chremnitz White\\
linseed oil and turpentine}

\hand{jo}{Pink night gown, brown hair, blond skin, white sheets with gray
shadows. Back\\
of head + figure in shadow. Cut window red brick upper stories. Blue sky.\\
\struck{Strip of vertical} strips of masonry left side of window, white in full
light. Room partly\\
in shadow green, light areas light green. End of window \struck{curtain} dark
green}

\marginal{sheets of --- above « with gray »; rim --- above « window » in the
third line; shade --- above the struck « curtain »; upper right --- above the
end of the last line}

\hand{jo}{Columbus Mus. Ap. 12, 54 \quad 3500 - 1/3}

\hand{jo}{May 14, 54 Paid on account by Columbus Mus \qquad 3500 selling price\\
\qquad less 1/3 \quad 1166 Commission\\
\qquad 2333.34 \struck{\ill{} May 4, 54}\\
\quad May 4, 54 Rec'd on account \quad 2000.\\
\quad Balance due - \qquad 333.34. \quad Paid July 9, 54.\\
\struck{1/3 \quad 117}}

\note{the only worked sum in the batch. Everywhere else she writes the price,
the fraction and the answer on one line; here she sets the selling price above
the commission, rules it off, carries the difference down, takes off a payment
on account and gives the balance and the day it was cleared. The first line is
in pencil under the ink and dates the purchase five weeks before the payment.
Two struck passages are in the column: a date beside 2333.34 that is crossed
through and illegible, and a fraction and a figure at the foot below the
balance. « on account » is inserted above « Paid » in the ink line, which is
what makes the two May dates a payment and a receipt rather than a
contradiction.}
