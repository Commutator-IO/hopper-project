% sheets by a human.
%
% Book III, batch 7: sheets 73 to 84 - the watercolour list runs out, and the
% volume ends in three kinds of leaf that have nothing to do with each other.
%
% Eight things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The watercolour list of batch 6 changes shape again inside this batch and
% then stops. Leaves 113 to 125 are one work to a leaf, not two: Edward's ink
% record drawing at the head, then the title and the size, then two or three
% lines of description, then the sale. That is the shape of the oil leaves of
% batches 4 and 5 with the paint formula taken out, and it is the shape of nine
% of the twelve sheets here. Then the foliation jumps from 125 to 170 and the
% last three sheets are a Whereabouts running list (leaves 170 and 171) and a
% four-line memorandum headed Frames (the leaf printed 187). Budget attention
% accordingly: the work leaves are quick and every one of their sales
% reconciles, and the two Whereabouts leaves are the densest prose in the
% volume - twenty-eight lines of it, in a hand travelling fast, with no
% arithmetic in it at all.
%
% Three of the twelve sheets are one leaf. Sheets 75, 76 and 77 are all leaf
% 117, and only the third of them shows it. Sheet 75 is a plain unruled sheet
% carrying the whole California Hills record - drawing, title, description,
% prize and purchase - lying over the leaf, with the leaf's printed 117 showing
% above its top edge; the Whitney's descriptor places it at p. 116 and the
% catalogue follows, so it takes \sheet{17794}{116}. Sheet 76 is the leaf with
% a second loose sheet, the gift drawing for Mrs. Rose, over its lower half,
% covering the Gloucester Beach entry down to three word-ends at the fore edge.
% Sheet 77 is the same recto with both moved aside. Following batch 1's rule,
% leaf 117 is transcribed once, at sheet 77, each laid sheet is transcribed as
% the document it is, and the sheets that cover it carry a \note{}. Nothing is
% transcribed twice.
%
% The foliation is the stationer's, printed in blue, and it is odd-numbered on
% a recto as it has been since leaf 9: 113, 115, 117, 119, 121, 123, 125, then
% 170, 171, then 187. The leaves between 125 and 170 and between 171 and 187
% were not photographed and nothing here says what is on them. Leaf 170 is an
% even number and carries its printed 170 at the top left, which is what a
% written verso does in this volume; the Whitney gives it a leaf number where
% batches 2 and 3 met versos it left unnumbered, so it takes \sheet{18199}{170}
% and not an empty second argument. The Frames leaf is the other way round: its
% printed 187 is plain at the top right and the Whitney's descriptor gives no
% leaf, so it is \sheet{17145}{} with the number in a \note{}.
%
% Every sale on the nine work leaves reconciles, and the third is taken as a
% fraction throughout: 1800 - 1/3 = 1200 twice, 1500 - 1/3 = 1000 twice,
% 900 - 1/3 = 600, 2000 - 1/3 = 1333 1/3, 6500 - 1/3 = 4333 2/3. That is the
% whole of the arithmetic in the batch and it is the cleanest run of it in the
% volume - batch 6's leaf 109, which took the same third twice and got 600 out
% of 750 both times, has no counterpart here. Two of the nine leaves carry no
% money at all: leaf 121, where the entry stops at the paper and the paint, and
% leaf 125, which is a drawing, a title, a size and a year.
%
% Leaf 121 is written in two hands and they do not look alike. The title, the
% studio note and the marginals are hers, running and impatient; the three
% lines below them - painted July, August 1962, Whatmans Cold Pressed water
% color paper, Winsor & Newton water colors - are upright, evenly spaced and
% separately pen-lifted, and they are the only paint formula in the batch. They
% are given to an unidentified hand rather than to hers or to Edward's, because
% nothing on the leaf settles it and the vocabulary exists so that a doubtful
% attribution does not fall on her by default.
%
% Her ordinal mark is everywhere on these leaves and it is the inch mark: the
% fourth visit to Mexico is « 4" visit », the sale dates are « July 2", 59 »
% and « July 6", 1960 », and leaf 171 hangs two pictures on « 3" fl. » and
% « 2" fl. » of the Whitney. Where the same mark closes a measurement it is an
% inch mark and means it - « 12" x 18" » on the gift drawing. Both are
% transcribed as written and neither is expanded.
%
% Leaf 170 is a running list she wrote as an account of two bad years and one
% good one, and it is the best prose in Book III so far: the dune top over the
% sea bulldozed away at South Truro, the arrival at 3 Washington Square of what
% she calls Hitler's aggressors, five studios turned into offices, and then
% eleven lines saying that the chaos made the whole house friends - the shared
% backaches, the plaster in the halls, the tenants who grieved when the workmen
% left, the Irish and Italian Catholics who were not voting the way anyone
% expected, and the radiators that arrived in time for Christmas. It is not
% ruled, it carries no figures, and it is transcribed as she wrote it.
%
% Six of the twelve sheets carry a correction written above the line with a
% caret under it, and on this batch the caret is reliable: « done » over the
% date on leaf 113, « the houses themselves » over the description on leaf 117,
% « House » between « pre-Civil War » and « with later additions » on leaf 119,
% « Spring » before « 1929 » on leaf 123, « its » over a struck « their » and
% « upper » before « balcony » on leaf 170, and « 40" » over a struck figure on
% leaf 171. Where the caret is there the addition is read in; where a remark
% simply stands above the line it is a \marginal{}.
