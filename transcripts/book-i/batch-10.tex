%
% Book I, batch 10: sheets 109 to 117, leaves 94 to 100 and the back flyleaf.
% This is the last batch of Book I. Nine sheets, and the last of them - the
% back cover - carries nothing but the stationer's blind-stamped ornament, so
% it does not appear below. Eight sheets are transcribed and every one of them
% is a leaf nobody has read before.
%
% Eight things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The batch finishes the index apparatus batch 9 opened and then stops. Leaves
% 94 and 95 are « Prizes, Museum Purchases », leaves 96, 97 and 98 are
% « Reviews, Reproductions », leaf 99 is « Gifts », leaf 100 is the list of
% people given a copy of the 1945 monograph, and the back flyleaf is
% « Summer whereabouts » - a chronology of where the Hoppers spent every year
% from 1923 to 1963, which is the last thing written in the volume and the
% latest hand in it.
%
% There is no \work{} anywhere in the file, for batch 9's reason. A title on
% these leaves is cited - it names a picture that won a prize, was reviewed,
% was given away - and is not the head of its own record. « Night Shadows »
% alone is cited some twenty times across leaves 94, 96, 97, 98 and 99.
%
% Batch 9 reported that ten consecutive leaves of Book I carried no money at
% all. That run ends here, and it ends on the one leaf nobody would look for it
% on. Leaf 100 carries, written sideways across the leaf so that the book must
% be turned to read it, two accounts in her hand: the expenses of the Rehn
% exhibition of February 1927 (advertising, window signs, photographs, frames,
% \$342.49 in all) and a settlement with F. Rehn of 4 November totalling
% \$1452.68. They are the only figures in the batch, and they are the last
% money in Book I. Everything else on leaves 94 to 100 is a prize, a notice or
% a gift.
%
% The three « Reviews, Reproductions » leaves run backwards. Leaf 96 opens
% « Continued from p. 97 », leaf 97 opens « Continued from p. 98 », and leaf 98
% closes « Cont. p. 97 » - so the text runs 98, then 97, then 96, and the run
% ends where it looks as though it begins. They are transcribed in the order
% they are bound, as batch 5 did for leaves 46/47 and 48/49. The reason is
% visible on the sheets: she filled the recto, then used the leaf before it for
% the overflow.
%
% Her ink underline changes register again, and it is not batch 9's red pencil.
% On leaves 84 to 91 a red pencil underlined the name of every exhibiting
% institution and was not transcribed. On leaves 94 and 95 she underlines, in
% the same brown ink she is writing with, both the institution and the title of
% the work - « Art Institute Chicago » and « East Side Interior » on one line,
% some ninety times in two leaves. It is hers, it is ink, and it is still a
% finding device rather than emphasis, so it is recorded here once and not
% transcribed line by line. \emph{} is kept for the headings she rules under at
% the head of a leaf, which is what batch 9 reserved it for.
%
% Nothing is pasted to any leaf of this batch. Every \ill{} in the file is
% therefore a word she scribbled out herself, a figure written over another, or
% a pencil line too faint to resolve at 6x - never a covered line, and never a
% line a second photograph might recover. The two worst are the last two lines
% of the back flyleaf, in a soft pencil over an earlier pencil, and the third
% « The \ill{} » on leaf 99.
%
% Her own queries are on the leaves and are transcribed as she wrote them:
% « Roofs ? / Sattillo. » on leaf 95, « (Mar. 1st ?) » on leaf 96, « a set of
% etchings (12?) » on leaf 99, « T.V. Ad ? » on the flyleaf. Batch 8 met this
% habit once, at leaf 74's « (spelling?) »; this batch has four.
%
% One spelling is worth flagging before the leaves rather than after. Royal
% Cortissoz, the Tribune's critic, is named three times in the batch and is
% spelt differently each time - « Cortisez » on leaf 97, a form on leaf 98 that
% will not settle, and « Cortisoz » nine lines below it on the same leaf.
% « Les Poilus » does the same: « Poiltus » on leaf 98, « Poilus » twice on
% leaf 99. Both are as written, both times.
