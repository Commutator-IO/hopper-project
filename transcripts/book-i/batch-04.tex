%
% PROVENANCE CAVEAT. The skill asks for a fresh context per batch, because a
% pass reading twelve leaves degrades towards the end with nothing to signal
% it. Batches 1 and 2 each had a context of their own; batches 3 and 4 shared
% one. Recorded here rather than left for a reader to discover.
%
% Book I, batch 4: sheets 37 to 48, leaves 34 to 44, and the two loose sheets
% found between leaves 44 and 45. This is the end of the plate-by-plate record:
% leaf 44 gathers the four small plates that never earned a leaf of their own,
% and from leaf 45 the volume becomes running lists.
%
% Three things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% Leaf 35 is not a continuation of leaf 34. It is a receipt: eleven etchings
% that F. R. - Frank Rehn - took from the studio on Sunday 27 May 1951, listed
% by name. It sits opposite Girl on a Bridge for no reason but that the leaf
% was blank.
%
% Leaf 44 carries five works and two much later transactions, including the
% Philadelphia Museum's purchase of a large collection of etchings and
% drypoints in February 1962 and a gift to the same museum of seven preliminary
% drawings, each with a valuation. Those valuations are the only figures in
% Book I that are not sale prices, and they are forty times the prices on the
% leaves around them.
%
% The two loose sheets are a different document altogether: descriptions of
% Hopper watercolours in John Taylor Spaulding's collection, written for the
% 1948 memorial exhibition at the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston. They describe
% pictures rather than record transactions - "Mast is left, centre; heavy rugs
% hang over the side in foreground, cables" - which is the clearest sight in
% the volume of what her descriptive habit was for.
