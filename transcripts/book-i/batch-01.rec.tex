\begin{summary}
Book I opens with a sentence that is the key to everything after it. On leaf 1,
in Josephine Hopper's hand: \emph{Notation in ink at right side of page is date
when check received by E.H. from the Rehn Gallery. Pencil note means when
picture sold to buyer.} Two columns of figures that look alike are being kept
apart --- what the dealer paid, and when the work left --- and every price in
the volume is read against that line.

What follows on leaf 1 is an index in two halves. The first half is a table of
contents for the volume, and it points outward as often as inward: to Book II
for the Paris oils, to Book III for the notes and the prizes, to Book IV for
the drawings, and once to « some notes pages 90 - 93 in older ledger », which
is a volume this archive does not hold. The second half is a list of the
etching plates --- twenty-seven of them --- each with a price, sometimes two,
and the leaf on which its record stands.

The two prices are the interesting part. Almost every plate carries a higher
figure and a lower one beside it, the second replacing the first: Evening Wind
at 30 and then 25, Monhegan Boat at 25 and then 18. What that records is a
price list revised downward, once, across the whole set --- and the leaves that
follow show the revision holding for twenty years and then reversing: on leaf 3
the entry of 20 June 1945 reads \emph{Price raised to 50 - last few prints of
Edition.}

Leaves 2 to 5 carry the first two plates. The pattern is the one the whole
volume repeats. A halftone reproduction of the plate is hinged at the head of
the leaf, where the paintings books would have Edward Hopper's ink sketch;
beneath it his one line of drawn lettering, the title and the plate size; and
then thirty or forty ruled lines in her hand, running from the first jury of
1921 to a Kennedy Galleries sale of 1961. The dealers recur --- Keppel, Rehn,
Weyhe, Kennedy, the Downtown Gallery, Milch, Babcock, Allison --- and so do the
societies that accepted or refused: the Brooklyn Society of Etchers, the
Chicago Society of Etchers, the Philadelphia Water Color Club, the National
Academy of Design.

Two things about the leaves are worth knowing before the records below are
read. The reproductions cover text: on leaf 2 the clipping hides the price and
buyer columns for every row from January 1921 to December 1922, and on leaf 4
it hides the exhibitions column for two years. Those rows are marked illegible
in the transcription, and they are illegible because they are under paper. And
the entries are frequently out of chronological order --- on leaf 4 a sale
receipted « Jan. 3, 1924 » stands against an exhibition of April 1926 --- which
is the leaf's order and is not corrected anywhere in this project.

\field{note}{Recorded on leaves 2, 3 and 5. Its plate size is repeated at the

is a coincidence of two plates of the same size or a slip is not settled. The

The buyers and dealers listed here are joined from the two leaves and are not
in one place on the paper.}
