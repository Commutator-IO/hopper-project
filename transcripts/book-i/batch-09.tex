% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human. Its own context, no batch before it in this conversation.
%
% Book I, batch 9: sheets 97 to 108, leaves 84 to 93. Two of the twelve sheets
% are openings that duplicate leaves photographed singly, so the batch covers
% ten leaves across twelve sheets.
%
% Seven things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The book changes subject completely, and this is the change batch 5 first
% saw coming. Leaves 2 to 44 are the plates, leaves 45 to 83 the works by year;
% leaves 84 to 93 are the book's own index apparatus, and no work has a record
% here at all. Leaves 84 to 89 are « Current Exhibitions », a running list of
% where the pictures went from 1923 to 1943. Leaves 90 and 91 are « Reviews,
% Reproductions » - what was printed about them. Leaves 92 and 93 are
% « Photographs » - the negatives, who made them and who holds them. There is
% no money anywhere in the batch. Not one price, not one commission, not one
% receipt: ten consecutive leaves of Book I that the accounts cannot use.
%
% Consequently there is no \work{} in this file. A title on these leaves is
% cited, not recorded - « Chop Suey » on leaf 88 is the name of a thing that
% travelled to St. Louis, not the head of its block - and opening a work's
% block over a citation would put the same picture's heading on twenty leaves.
%
% Almost nothing is ruled into columns, and only one table is set. She writes
% these leaves as running lines with a date at the left, and braces the titles
% that belong to one exhibition with a large hand-drawn brace spanning three or
% four lines. Braces are given as « { » at the head of each braced line, which
% is where she drew them. The single ledgertable in the file is leaf 92's
% numbered list of McKillop negatives, which is genuinely two parallel columns
% of number and title; its header row is empty because the leaf heads nothing,
% and no heading is supplied in \add{} anywhere in this batch.
%
% Her red pencil is doing something here it has not done before. Batches 2 to 8
% found it ringing a figure or underlining a total. On leaves 84 to 91 it
% underlines the name of every exhibiting institution - Rehn, the Carnegie,
% the Modern Museum, the Fogg - and nothing else. It is a finding device laid
% over a list, added after the ink, and it is not transcribed: \emph{} is kept
% for the ink underlines she rules under her own headings, and a reader must be
% able to tell the two apart.
%
% Leaves 82 and 83 are not in the Whitney's sequence. Leaf 81 is photographed
% (batch 8), then the opening of 80 and 81, then leaf 84. Whether the two
% leaves are blank, missing or simply not photographed cannot be told from
% these images, and nothing here should be read as covering them.
%
% Two printed clippings are pasted in, and one of them costs the batch. The
% Rehn January 1929 checklist on leaf 87 lies clear of the writing and is
% transcribed whole - and is the most useful single object in the batch,
% because it prints in type the names she abbreviates in ink. The Rehn
% February 1927 checklist on leaf 85 is pasted flat across the middle of the
% leaf and covers a run of lenders' names; what shows past its edge at the
% fore-edge is given, every \ill{} there means « under paper », and there is no
% second photograph of leaf 85 anywhere in the Whitney's sequence.
%
% The 1929 clipping settles four names against her own spellings, on this
% batch's own evidence rather than from outside: it prints « Mr. John T.
% Spaulding » where leaf 84 writes « J. N. Spaulding » and leaf 85 « J. N.
% Spalding »; « Dr. & Mrs. H. H. M. Lyle » against hand.md's Lisle/Lyle pair;
% « Mr. & Mrs. Lesley G. Sheafer » into the standing Sheaffer tangle; « Marty
% Welch's House » and « Cape Ann Pasture » where leaf 92 writes « Marty
% Welsh's » and « Cape Anne Pasture ». All of them stay as each writes them.
% Leaf 93 also settles, in her own emphatic ink, the title hand.md has carried
% since batch 5: « 2 on the Aisle », written heavily over an erasure, against
% « Two on the Isle » on leaves 55 and 86.
