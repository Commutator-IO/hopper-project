% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human. Its own context, no batch before it in this conversation.
%
% Book I, batch 5: sheets 49 to 60, leaves 45 to 55. Leaf 53 is photographed
% twice and counts as two sheets, so the batch covers eleven leaves.
%
% This is where Book I changes what it is. Batches 1 to 4 ran one etching to a
% leaf; from leaf 45 the volume becomes running lists, and the subject changes
% with them - from the plates to the paintings, and from single transactions to
% the whole apparatus of a career: which shows, which dealers, which museums,
% and how much of each price the gallery took.
%
% Four things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The leaves do not run forwards. Leaf 46 opens « Continued from p. 47 » and
% leaf 48 opens « From p. 49 » - so two consecutive pairs were written back to
% front, the recto filled first and the facing verso used for the overflow.
% Neither is a mistake and neither has been reordered here.
%
% Leaf 45 is the only page in Book I that explains a picture rather than
% recording it: two hundred words on why Ex Lax was briefly called Es Lax, a
% laxative manufacturer's blessing, and the discrepancy between two
% photographs. It is also where the first oil sold - out of the 1913 Armory
% Show, for $250 less ten per cent - is written down, twenty years late.
%
% Leaf 53 cannot be read whole from either photograph, and this is not the
% usual clipping problem. The New York Pavements halftone is hinged: on sheet
% 18323 it lies flat and covers the Bootleggers block entirely, and on sheet
% 17852 it has been turned back to the left, where it lands on the date
% column. So the block's exhibition dates survive nowhere - fragments of four
% of them are visible at the fore-edge and nothing else. Both photographs are
% given a \sheet{}; the transcription is at 17852, where all but the dates can
% be read.
%
% The prices here are an order of magnitude above the etchings. A plate sold
% for $18 to $50; the oils on leaves 52 to 55 sell at $400 to $1500, and one
% resells at $2250 in 1957. The commission is written as she wrote it -
% « 1500 - 1/3 Com. = 1000 » - and is never worked out here.

\hand{jo}{EX LAX, p. 55. Concerning name: It was thought the title, as name of
well known laxative might create prejudice against picture --- (as little grey
structure in back of house, but painted head on in Two Lights Village --- water
color --- caused Dr. \uncertain{Slyple} to return it because Mrs. L. did'nt
care for such permanent treatment of a privy) It was suggested that E. Hopper
change name to ES LAX. But the 2 X's in Ex Lax compose so much better, E. H.
just made the change in water color. So soon after this, the canvas was bought
by J. Spalding (Boston) who had no scruples about EX LAX, so water color S ---
removed. But in meantime the picture had been photographed as Es Lax, \&
reproduced somewhere, \& discovered by the Ex Lax people themselves, who wrote
asking that the right name be used with their blessing. Later photographs have
the Ex Lax. This to explain any confusion about the 2 photographs.}
