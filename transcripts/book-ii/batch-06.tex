% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human. Its own context, no batch before it in this conversation.
%
% Book II, batch 6: sheets 61 to 72, leaves 79, 81, 83, 85, 87, 89, 91, 93, 95,
% 97 and 99, and the back cover. Leaves 80 to 98 even were not photographed and
% neither was 78; every leaf in the batch is a recto, the foliation runs odd
% throughout, and the last sheet is the cover. This is where Book II ends.
%
% Six things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The volume is running forwards again, and in one direction. Batch 5 went
% backwards thirty years into the Paris canvases of 1907; batch 6 is eleven
% leaves of oils finished between February 1940 and April 1942, laid down in
% the order they were painted - Office at Night, Light Battery at Gettysburg,
% Gas, House by a Road, Girlie Show, Route 6 thru Eastham, Lee Shore, Night
% Hawks, Dawn in Pennsylvania. Only leaf 87 breaks it, and it says why: Corner
% Saloon was painted in 1913 and the leaf records what happened to it in 1941,
% when it went to the Rehn Gallery for the show of early work that batch 5's
% leaf 74 is the checklist of. Leaf 74's right-hand column says « See page 87 »,
% and this is the page it meant.
%
% The paint formula is a standing field on every one of these leaves. Batch 1
% found it beside the title and warned that it is neither description nor
% marginal; batch 6 has it ten times out of ten, and it is the most regular
% thing in the batch: Blockx and Winsor & Newton colors, a named white - Blockx
% Silver White seven times, W & N Zinc white for Night Hawks, W & N Flake white
% for Dawn in Pennsylvania - poppy oil or, once, linseed, and then the canvas.
% The canvases are Belgian double prime, Winton, English linen with domestic
% priming, and on leaf 89 a maker's name struck out, which is the word batch 2
% read « Fornet » on leaf 31 and this leaf writes « Foinet ».
%
% She is still writing in pencil first and inking over the erasure. Batch 5
% found this on leaves 60, 62, 69 and 77, where it took the titles; here it
% takes the whole head. On leaves 91 and 97 an erased pencil draft of the
% title, the size and the entire paint formula stands under the ink and can be
% read through it; leaf 87 has an erased « Corner Saloon » above the inked one,
% and leaf 89 an erased line above the title that cannot.
%
% Leaf 83's record drawing is not drawn on the leaf. It is on a separate sheet
% of paper adhered across the head, with « 26 x 40 » in pencil below the
% drawing, and its own edges show at the top and at the fore-edge. What it
% covers is the strip where leaves 89, 91, 93, 95 and 97 all carry a pencil
% year, so a heading may or may not be under it - nothing about the sheet
% settles that, and no second photograph of this leaf exists.
%
% Two commissions, four leaves and one day apart, and only one of them works.
% Leaf 81 books the Kansas City sale at « 1275 - 1/3 = 850 », which is exactly
% two thirds; leaf 85 books Mrs. Rand at « 1250 - 1/3 = 850 », where two thirds
% is 833 1/3. Both figures are unambiguous at 7x and both cheques are dated
% Dec. 29, 47. Every other commission in the batch reconciles. The figures are
% transcribed and the arithmetic is left where she put it.
%
% Six \ill{} marks, and not one is under paper - nothing in these twelve sheets
% is pasted down except the drawing sheet on leaf 83, which covers no writing
% that can be shown to exist. Two are words she scribbled over (leaf 79, leaf
% 99), two are figures written one on top of another in the Walker Art Center
% line of leaf 79, and two are cancelled endings. The Whitney's descriptors
% disagree with three of the leaves: it calls leaf 93 Sea Shore where the leaf
% is headed Lee Shore, leaf 91 Route 6 where the leaf writes Route 6 thru
% Eastham, and leaf 99 « Photographs- » where the leaf goes on to name the
% photographer.
