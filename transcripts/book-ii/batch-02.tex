% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book II, batch 2: sheets 13 to 24, leaves 11, 13, 14, 15, 17, 19, 21, 23, 25,
% 27, 29 and 31. Eleven rectos and one verso.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The leaf numbers in Book II are printed, not written. Every leaf of this
% batch carries its number in blue stationer's type at the outer corner - right
% on a recto, left on a verso - which is why the batch runs 11, 13, 15 and not
% 11, 12, 13: the even leaves are blank versos the Whitney did not photograph.
% Leaf 14 is the one exception, and it is in the batch because somebody wrote
% on it. This is a real difference from Book I, where the Hoppers numbered the
% leaves themselves and a sheet without a number is a sheet nobody numbered.
%
% Leaf 14 is a facing page used for overflow, and it runs backwards. The
% Circle Theatre's record is on leaf 15 and dated 1936; leaf 14, which sits to
% its left in the opening, carries what happened to the picture in 1950 and
% 1951 - the Whitney returning it for \$500 against 7 A.M., and Stein buying it
% at once for 2500. So the leaves are transcribed in the order they are bound
% and the content of the earlier one post-dates the later by fourteen years.
% Book I's leaves 46 and 48 are the same phenomenon; this is its first
% appearance in Book II.
%
% The title block migrates down the batch, and the drift is chronological.
% Leaves 11 to 19 head the leaf with title, date and size at the top left and
% put Edward Hopper's record drawing below. From leaf 21 the drawing moves to
% the left and the title block sits beside it on the right. From leaf 27 the
% title is written underneath the drawing. Nothing about the record changes;
% only where she starts writing.
%
% Nothing is pasted to any leaf in this batch. That makes it the first Book II
% batch with no illegible mark meaning « under paper »: all six here are marks
% that genuinely cannot be read, five of them under her own cancellation and
% one an erased pencil line on leaf 11.
%
% She corrects above the line throughout, as in batch 3, and each correction is
% given as a marginal note naming the words it stands over. The subject is
% eleven oils, and unlike anything in Book I they are in strict order: The Long
% Leg of November 1935 through to Cape Cod Evening of July 1939, eleven works
% in eleven consecutive rectos with nothing out of sequence. They sell through
% Rehn at 900 to 4500 less a third, to the Whitney, Newark, the Carnegie, the
% Art Institute, Wichita and Stephen C. Clark, and Cape Cod Afternoon takes the
% Corcoran's first prize and \$2000 before the Carnegie buys it. One is not
% sold at all: Jo Painting was given to its sitter, who is the person keeping
% the book.
