% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book II, batch 1: sheets 1 to 12 - the front cover, the inside front cover,
% the index on leaf 1, and then leaves 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9. Two of the
% twelve are second photographs of a leaf already in the batch.
%
% Six things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The leaf numbers do not run odd at the front of the book. Batch 2 found
% Book II running 11, 13, 15 and inferred that the even leaves are blank versos
% the Whitney did not photograph. That does not hold here: leaves 2, 3, 4, 5, 6
% and 7 are consecutive, and only leaf 8 is missing from the sequence. So the
% odd-only pattern is something the volume acquires later rather than a fact
% about its foliation, and « leaves 2-9 » beside « sheets 1-12 » is correct.
%
% Two hinged clippings are photographed twice, and the Whitney calls the second
% photograph a verso. Sheet 18397 is called « [p.4] - Verso » and sheet 17606
% « [p. 6] - Verso »; what each photographs is the recto again, with the
% clipping turned back off the text. Batch 2 met this at leaf 9 and set the
% rule: give both sheets a \sheet{}, transcribe once at the one that shows
% more, and let the note say what the other hides. Leaves 4 and 6 are
% transcribed at 18397 and 17606 accordingly.
%
% Leaf 7 is the case where there is no second photograph. The Virginia Museum
% bulletin laid across its foot covers everything below the third line of the
% description - the rest of the description, the whole of the sale - and
% nothing in this batch turns it back. Every \ill{} on that leaf is text under
% paper, and four fragments beyond the clipping's right edge are all that
% survive of four separate lines.
%
% The title block does not drift steadily. Batch 2 read a chronological drift
% through leaves 11 to 31 - title above the drawing, then drawing left and
% title right, then title underneath. The front of the book does not lead into
% that: leaf 2 heads the leaf and puts the drawing at the right, leaf 5 puts
% the drawing at the left with the block beside it, and leaves 6 and 9 head the
% leaf again. Read the head of each leaf.
%
% The inside front cover carries Book II's own version of the statement Book I
% makes, and it is shorter: « Recorded by Jo N. Hopper (Mrs. Edward Hopper.)
% before each work leaves studio. Drawings by Edward Hopper. » It is in her
% hand, and so is the framed address block above it - which is a difference
% from Book I, whose cover and title page are drawn lettering that batch 1
% recorded as unidentified.
%
% She writes « ± » for « about », twice: « Ap. 9 ± 1961 » on leaf 3 and
% « ± 1929 » in the index. The mark is hers and is transcribed as it stands.
%
% The subject is the oils of 1932 to 1935 - eleven works from the two South
% Truro houses of October 1932 through to Shakespeare at Dusk of November 1935
% - and the paint is recorded as consistently as the sales: zinc white,
% Rembrandt or « R. » colors, poppy oil, on nearly every leaf. They sell to
% Huntington Hartford, Randolph Macon Women's College, the National Academy's
% Ranger Fund, Edward G. Robinson, the Virginia Museum and John Jacob Astor,
% and one is given away: Cape Cod Sunset went to the person keeping the book.
