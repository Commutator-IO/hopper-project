% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human. Its own context, no batch before it in this conversation.
%
% Book II, batch 4: sheets 37 to 48, leaves 45, 46, 47, 49, 51, 53, 55, 57 and
% 58, plus three sheets that carry no leaf number - two detached bifolia of
% pencil drawings and one hinged sheet. Leaf 46 is the only verso in the batch
% that was written on; 48, 50, 52, 54 and 56 were not photographed.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The shape of the leaf has changed. Batches 2 and 3 gave one work to a leaf -
% a title, a size, ten or fifteen sentences of description, one sale line. From
% leaf 45 the leaf holds two or three works, and each of them gets four or five
% lines: the title, the size, the color, the buyer and the check. The
% description has stopped being an anecdote about who is in the picture and
% become a note of what color everything is, which is what a watercolour needs
% and an oil apparently did not. The subject is the Cape Cod and Vermont
% watercolours of 1936 to 1942 - South Truro, Eastham, Wellfleet, the White
% River at Royalton and Sharon - with one western leaf, 1941, from the trip
% that produced Oregon Coast and Shoshone Cliffs.
%
% Three sheets of this batch are not leaves of the book at all, and they are
% the most useful thing in it. Sheets 17036 and 16499 are detached bifolia
% carrying six of Edward Hopper's record drawings in pencil rather than ink,
% each captioned by Jo Hopper with the title, the size, the owner and - four
% times - the page of Book II where the entry stands. « Book II p. 47 »,
% « Book II p. 49 » twice, and so on. They are the answer to leaf 47, whose
% first drawing box is empty and reads « See opposite page for pictures ». The
% third, sheet 17379, is a hinged sheet with an ink drawing of The Captain's
% House and a caption dating it to Gloucester, 1924 - a picture from fifteen
% years before the leaves it sits among.
%
% Where the detached sheet and the leaf disagree, both readings stand. Route
% 14, Vermont went to a benefit auction: leaf 47 says « Finish Relief » and
% sheet 17036 says « Given to Polish Relief », and the buyer is « Phillip
% Goodwin » then « Philip Goodwin » on leaf 47, « Goodwin » on leaf 46 and
% « Goodman? Philip » on the detached sheet. Nothing is regularised and nothing
% is chosen between.
%
% She corrects above the line here as she did in batches 2 and 3, and every
% such word is given as a \marginal{} after the passage it stands over, saying
% which words it sits above. Two of them are struck alternatives rather than
% corrections - « oblique » over « horizontal » on leaf 45, « Jacob H. » over a
% struck « Jacob » on leaf 55.
%
% Nothing in this batch is pasted down and nothing is illegible under paper.
% Both \ill{} marks are her own cancellations - the word in quotation marks
% under Youngstown on leaf 57, the figure struck after « Gloucester 1924 » on
% the hinged sheet - scribbled over rather than struck through once, and
% neither resolves at any magnification. A third such mark, the word struck
% after « dark » on leaf 49, is given as \uncertain{} because enough of it
% survives to offer a reading.
