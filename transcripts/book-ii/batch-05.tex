% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-06 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human. Its own context, no batch before it in this conversation.
%
% Book II, batch 5: sheets 49 to 60, leaves 60, 61, 62, 63, 68, 69, 70, 71, 73,
% 74, 75 and 77. Leaves 64 to 67, 72 and 76 were not photographed, and neither
% was 59; every sheet in the batch is a recto and none is a verso, a cover or a
% loose insertion.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The volume has gone backwards by thirty years. Batch 4 ended in the Cape Cod
% and Vermont watercolours of 1936 to 1942. Batch 5 opens on « Paris - Early
% Oils 1907 - 1910 » and stays in the early work for nine of its twelve leaves:
% the nine Paris canvases of 1907 and 1909, then the American oils of 1908 to
% 1914 - Railroad Train, Squam Light, El Station, Moonlight Interior, American
% Village - then Bootleggers of 1924 or 1925 and East River of about 1929. Only
% leaf 77, The MacArthurs' Home of 1939, belongs to the years the batch before
% it was recording. These are leaves written up long after the fact, and the
% dates carry her query marks: « 1907? », « 1909? », « 1913 or before »,
% « bet. 1920-23 ».
%
% Leaves 60 to 63 carry no money at all. They are four leaves of Edward
% Hopper's record drawings, three to a leaf, with nothing beside them but a
% title, a size, a year and a sentence about how pale the paint is. Nine
% pictures, no buyer, no price, no date of sale - the only transactions on them
% are two gifts to herself in 1957. Leaf 63 rules a third drawing box at the
% foot and leaves it empty, and nothing is written beside it.
%
% Leaf 74 is the one ruled table in the batch, and it indexes the rest of it.
% It is the checklist of the Rehn Gallery's « Edward Hopper 1907-1914 » of
% January 1941 - fourteen oils, then the nine Paris sketches under the heading
% « Sketches pp. 60 - 63 - Bk. II », then a pencil list of pictures that did not
% make the show. Its right-hand column carries page references back into this
% same volume - « Page 68 », « See p. 69 », « Page 70 », « See page 87 » - and
% the annotations were added over at least fifteen years, so a 1941 checklist
% ends up recording a sale of December 1951.
%
% Where the leaf and the checklist disagree, both readings stand. Le Louvre et
% la Sëine is « 23 1/2 x 28 3/4 » on leaf 60 and « 23 1/2 x 28 1/2 » on leaf 74;
% American Village is 1913 on leaf 70 and 1912 on leaf 74; Le Pavillon de Flore
% on leaf 62 is Le Pavillion de Flore on leaf 74; and she writes « Sëine » with
% a diaeresis on leaf 60 and « Seine » without one on leaf 62. Nothing is
% regularised and nothing is chosen between.
%
% She writes over her own pencil. On leaves 60 and 62 each title was set down
% in pencil, rubbed out and written again in ink, and the erased pencil is
% still legible under and above the ink - most plainly at the head of leaf 60,
% where a whole erased « Le Louvre et la Seine » stands above the inked one.
% The same thing happens twice on leaf 69, where an erased « 1913 Corner Saloon
% See p. 87 » stands under the heading and a second erased copy of it sits
% inside the empty drawing box, and once on leaf 77, where an erased « Dec 21st
% 1939 » stands beside the inked « Dec. 21'', 39. » Those erasures are given as
% transcribed text with a note, not as \ill{}, because they can be read.
%
% Three \ill{} marks, none of them under paper - nothing in these twelve sheets
% is pasted down. All three are struck words of her own: after « Squam Light »
% and after « El Station » on leaf 69, and after « Roof » on leaf 71. A mark
% that will not resolve at 2x will not resolve at 5x either, because there is
% nothing under the ink to resolve. The pencil she wrote and struck at the head
% of leaf 68 is not among them - that one can be read.
