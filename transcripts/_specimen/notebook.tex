% This file exists to show the shape a transcription of one of Josephine

% end to end. It transcribes nothing: the words are invented, and the two
% resource refs are the front cover and page 1 of « Battle of Wash Sq. » only
% so that the renderer's archive check has something real to check against.
% Never place a file like this under transcripts/notebooks/ - six months on,
% nothing would distinguish it from a reading.

\section{Front cover}

\sheet{89298}{}
A notebook's cover carries her title, and the transcription gives it as
written: \hand{jo}{Battle of Wash Sq.}

\section{Page 1}

\sheet{89299}{1}
A notebook is read in entries, not in rows. Each begins with the entry macro:
the date as she wrote it first, then the date the transcriber assigns it, as
much of one as the page allows, and the second is the one claim here that is
ours.

\entry{Tuesday}{1947-03-11}
\hand{jo}{This is not a sentence she wrote. It stands where her first entry
would, to show that prose runs on under the date, that a word she struck is
\struck{gone}, that a word that will not be read is \ill{}, and that a
reading offered doubtfully is \uncertain{doubtful}.}

\note{the transcriber's note, and it is ours: what a reader of the record
needs and the page itself does not say}

\entry{Wed.}{1947-03-12}
\hand{jo}{A second entry, one day on, to show that the date column of a
ledger has become the head of an entry here, and that an entry can run over
the page-turn without being restated.}

\marginal{a note written in the margin of the notebook, and it is hers}
