% Dealers/Etchings, batch 2: twelve sheets, leaves 61 to 74. All twelve carry
% writing, all twelve are transcribed, no opening is photographed twice, and -
% as in batch 1 - there is not one clipping in the batch.
%
% Three things about these twelve leaves.
%
% First, the batch spans leaves 61 to 74 but only twelve sheets, because leaves
% 64 and 65 were not photographed. The printed numbers run 61, 62, 63, then 66:
% the gap is in the Whitney's sequence, not in the book, and nothing here says
% what is on the two missing leaves.
%
% Second, leaves 62 and 63 are written SIDEWAYS, and they are written sideways
% across the opening as one landscape surface - the writing runs out of the
% foot of 62 and into the head of 63, and each photograph catches a band of the
% facing leaf beyond the gutter. Both photographs must be turned a quarter turn
% clockwise to be read. These two leaves are also the only ones in the volume
% so far that are not about Edward Hopper at all: they are Josephine Hopper's
% record of her own watercolours, her own dealers and her own exhibitions,
% including the ones that turned her down.
%
% Third, from leaf 69 the volume becomes what leaf 51's index said it would be.
% Leaf 70 is Keppel, 71 is Keppel continued, 72 is Kraushaar, 73 is Kennedy and
% then Denko, 74 is Randolph - one dealer to a leaf, each with a consignment
% table running twenty or thirty rows, and each ending in a pointer to a page
% further in. Those pointers - « over, p. 72 », « See page 84 », « Continued on
% p. 85 » - are the volume telling a reader how to use it.

\section{Leaves 62 and 63: Josephine Hopper's own watercolours}

\note{leaves 62 and 63 are written sideways, and they are written sideways
across the opening as a single landscape surface: the left-hand columns run out
of the foot of leaf 62 and continue at the head of leaf 63 without a break, and
so do the right-hand ones. Each of the two photographs takes in a band of the
facing leaf beyond the gutter, so several lines appear twice; each is
transcribed once, on the leaf it is written on. Both sheets must be turned a
quarter turn clockwise to be read.}

\note{nothing on these two leaves concerns Edward Hopper's work. They are Josephine
Nivison Hopper's record of her own watercolours --- what she showed, what she
priced them at, what sold, what was returned, and, in the long run of entries
from 1938 on, which juries threw her out. The record is hers throughout, and
the two hands of the volume are here one hand writing about herself.}
