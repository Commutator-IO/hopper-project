% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 13: sheets 145 to 156, which are leaves 144 to 155, and carry
% five years and eleven months from 2 January 1959 to 7 November 1964. Every
% sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions and
% no blanks. One leaf carries paper that is not the leaf: a slip of white paper
% stuck over the foot of the date column of leaf 154, with the last receipt's
% date written on it.
%
% This is the batch that was skipped when Book IV was read through, so it is
% the last of the volume to be transcribed rather than the last in the book.
% Batch 12 ends at leaf 143 and batch 14 opens at leaf 156; these twelve leaves
% close the gap between them.
%
% Ten things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns are still the stationer's and still unheaded, as they have been
% since leaf 7, and the hand of the entries is the one hand of the twelve
% earlier batches. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the writer, so the
% batch is attributed to no one.
%
% The colour rule holds throughout. Charges are black, receipts red and sums
% pencil, as they have been since leaf 7. There are three exceptions and each
% is worth its note: leaf 145's whole commission block is in pencil rather than
% black; leaf 153's first entry, the fire insurance on the Buick, is in pencil
% throughout including its receipt line, where red would be expected; and leaf
% 151 carries pencil ovals drawn round two pairs of figures and pencil crosses
% in the left margin beside the entries they belong to, which are marks of
% selection by somebody reading the book rather than keeping it.
%
% The two digit traps of batch 14 are already here, five years earlier, and the
% arithmetic settles them every time. Leaf 146's Rehn sale reads 10,800.88 and
% 3,833.33 letter by letter and is 10,000.00 and 3,333.33, because the net
% 6,666.67 written beneath is legible and is the difference; leaf 151's red
% cheque reads 10000.40 and is 10000.00; leaf 153 writes « less 83 1/3 % » for
% « less 33 1/3 % » and its own 31,975.00 less 10658.00 gives the 21,317.00
% ruled off. Her 0 takes a closed entry loop at the head of a run and her 3
% carries a closed upper loop, and either can pass for an 8.
%
% Leaf 149 gives the cleanest control on that 3 in the volume so far. The net
% of the Allison print sale is written twice on consecutive lines, 236.67 in
% black and 236.67 in red, and the same digit in the same position of the same
% figure is the closed-loop form in one ink and the plain open form in the
% other. It is the shape of leaf 159's 28.83 against 28.23, four years earlier.
%
% Four sums do not follow from the figures above them, and they are not alike.
% Leaf 145's cheque of 4791.79 is four cents more than 7290.00 less the 2498.25
% ruled off, and the pencil carry beneath it uses 4791.79, so the leaf is
% consistent with itself and not with its own subtraction. Leaf 147's seven
% items make 6010 against a subtotal of 5960, and every figure after the
% subtotal follows the subtotal - the fifty is the etching entered at 80.00
% where the other etching on the leaf, immediately beneath it and plainly a
% different glyph, is 30.00. Leaf 150's second block rules off 5750.00, takes
% off 16.07 of photographs and pays 5633.93, which is a hundred short of the
% 5733.93 the subtraction gives, in black and in red alike. And leaf 146's
% pencil year total of 6686.40 is a dollar short of the 6687.40 its own two
% receipts make, the slip of leaves 83, 86 and 130.
%
% Two year totals are exact and one is not, and the one that is not says
% something. 1961 comes to 15637.21 on leaf 150 over three leaves, and 1962 to
% 39842.56 on leaf 152 over three more, both to the cent. 1960's 10656.24 on
% leaf 146 is the dollar-short sum above, and it also leaves out the Art in
% America award of 1000.00 that leaf 148 receipts on 20 December 1960. So does
% 1962's total leave out the 375.00 of fire insurance receipted eight days
% later. Prize money is counted in one year and left out of another, and
% insurance money is left out; the book applies the rule leaf 116 wrote down
% and does not write it again.
%
% The buyers go on being named, as they began to be in batch 12, and now they
% are named in the charge line itself rather than in the margin: Charles Stein,
% Chalmers, Mrs Sterling Morton for the Santa Barbara Museum, the Atlantic Art
% Club, Nathanson, Alfredo Valente, Mr and Mrs Albert Hackett, Lawrence
% Fleischman, the Butler Institute of American Art, and John Clancy buying for
% himself twice on leaf 154 - the Rehn manager who first wrote his way into the
% book on leaf 131.
%
% One entry runs across two leaves and one commission is never written. Leaf
% 154 charges « Road and Trees » at 15,000.00 on 6 July 1963, receipts 7,500.00
% on account the day before the charge, then charges the same picture at
% 15,000.00 again on 27 December and receipts 2,500.00 as the balance. The two
% receipts make 10,000.00, which is two thirds of the charge, and no commission
% line stands anywhere in either entry.
%
% The book starts citing dates as numerals. Leaf 152 deducts « payment on
% acount 1/24/1962 » from a sale of June 1962, pointing back to leaf 150's
% entry of 24 January; leaf 153 deducts « Jan 22, 62 Payment on Account
% Hackett » from a sale of January 1963. Both are the shape of leaf 128's
% « refer to statement of Dec 29, 1951 » - an entry that answers to something
% outside its own year.
%
% The last leaf carries a rubbed-out pencil figure at its foot where a year
% total would fall. Its four receipts make 1319.98. Nothing of the figure
% survives at 800\%, so it stands as \ill{} and the arithmetic is left in this
% note where it belongs.
