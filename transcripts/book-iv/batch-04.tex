% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 4: sheets 37 to 48, which are leaves 36 to 47, and carry
% twenty months of illustration income from 19 September 1918 to 19 May 1920.
% Every sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no
% insertions, no blanks and no clippings.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The book has not changed shape and neither have its columns. The leaf comes
% ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for the date, two more at the
% right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is written above any of
% them anywhere in the volume. The four columns are therefore left unheaded
% here, as they were in batches 1 to 3. The cents column is not used at all in
% this batch: every figure on these twelve leaves is a round dollar entered
% with the cents cell left blank, and those cells are blank on the leaf.
%
% The hand is the one hand of batches 1 to 3, unchanged: a fluent sloping
% copperplate, neither the small upright running hand nor the drawn title
% lettering of Books I to III. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the
% writer, and no signed comparison has been made, so every passage is
% \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The colour convention set on leaf 7 holds with two exceptions. The entries
% are in black and every receipt line - its date, its words and its figure - is
% in red; where a bill is rendered rather than paid, the line stays black. The
% exceptions are both worth having: on leaf 46 the date of a charge line is
% written in red although its figure is black, and on leaf 44 a whole entry -
% the De Vinne Press - is in pencil rather than ink, with only the year « 1920 »
% beside it in a heavy black hand. Two other pencil marks in the batch are not
% entries: the year « 1919 » above the date of 16 January on leaf 38, and an
% interlineation of two or three characters above the price of the Everybodys'
% drawing on leaf 39 that will not resolve at 800\%.
%
% The clients turn over a third time, and this is the turn out of war work. The
% Morse Dry Dock \& Repair Co. runs the whole batch, buying a two-colour cover
% for the Dry Dock Dial - the issue's month written above the line at the end of
% the description, as batch 3 found - at 35 in September 1918, at 40 from
% December, and at 45 from October 1919. Wells Fargo \& Co, who carried batch 3,
% do not appear; from October 1918 the Messenger covers are billed instead to
% the Am. Railway Express, at 40 apiece. What is new is periodical work at
% prices the volume has not seen: Scribners Magazine pays 100 and then 175,
% Everybodys' 75 and then 100, Colliers' 60. Adventure Magazine places one last
% list of thirteen line drawings and stops. The war charities of batch 3 give
% way to The World Outlook, The Centenary, Association Men and War Camp
% Community Service, and then from January 1920 to a run of commercial work -
% the Greeley Square Hotel, the Sperry Gyroscope Co., La France, the Christian
% Herald through J. W. Thompson.
%
% The arithmetic reconciles here more completely than in any earlier batch, and
% it settles several figures the ink alone would not. Thirteen Adventure titles
% at 15 make the 195 ruled off on leaf 37 and receipted on 7 November - three of
% the thirteen stand on leaf 36 and ten on leaf 37, so the sum is the only thing
% that binds the two leaves. On leaf 37 the second Everybodys' figure reads as
% 58 at page size and is 50 at 600\%, which is what 75 less 25 wants. The 65 of
% 21 January on leaf 39 is 40 plus 25 on the two lines above it; the 50 of 27
% January on leaf 38 is 10 plus 40; the 80 receipted on 9 July on leaf 41 answers
% two bills of 40 rendered a week apart, the first of which is left unreceipted
% where it stands. Every bill in the batch is answered to the dollar with two
% exceptions, and both are recorded below: the struck entry of 20 on leaf 47,
% which is crossed through and never paid, and the De Vinne Press sketch on leaf
% 44, where a pencil interlineation says 25 and the pencil bill and the red
% receipt below it both say 10.

\note{the four columns are the stationer's and not the writer's, as they are
throughout the volume: the leaf comes ruled with a red vertical rule at the
left for the date and two more at the right dividing dollars from cents, and
no heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are
therefore left unheaded here. The entries are in black and every receipt line
--- date, words and figure --- is in red.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch, and is the hand of batches 1 to 3:
a fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters and long descenders, unlike
either the small upright running hand or the drawn title lettering recorded
from Books I to III. Nothing on these sheets identifies the writer and no
signed comparison has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one.}

\note{the Adventure Magazine entry names no number of drawings --- « line
drawings » stands where the other lists of this kind in Book IV give a count
--- and its titles run over the leaf-turn: three stand here and ten more on
leaf 37, where a rule closes the group at 195. Thirteen titles at 15 make that
figure exactly, and the sum is the only thing on either leaf that joins the two
halves of the list.}

letter alone.}

sketch of 29 December is receipted on 19 March, and the two entries that follow
it are dated 10 and 12 January and receipted in January and February --- so two
