% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 1: sheets 1 to 12 - a used envelope folded round the book, a
% sheet of blank chipboard, the front cover, and leaves 3 to 11, which carry
% fifteen months of illustration income from 15 November 1913 to 11 February
% 1915.
%
% Six things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% This volume is not shaped like any of the others. It is a stationer's pocket
% memorandum book, and its pages come ruled with a money column already divided
% into dollars and cents by two printed red rules. Nobody adds a column to it
% and nobody heads one. Every leaf in the batch is the same four fields in the
% same order - the date at the left, the payer and what was done for him in the
% body, the dollars and the cents at the right - so the headings are supplied
% nowhere in this file and the note on leaf 3 says what the columns are. Where a
% figure stands in the body rather than in the money column it is a running
% subtotal, and it is transcribed where it stands.
%
% Sheet 2 does not appear. It is a piece of blank chipboard folded round the
% book, and there is nothing on it to transcribe.
%
% The batch is one hand from end to end: a fluent, sloping copperplate,
% unlike either the small upright running hand or the drawn title lettering
% recorded from Books I to III. The entries begin in November 1913, eleven
% years before Josephine Nivison entered the account, and no second hand joins
% them anywhere in these twelve sheets. Nothing here settles whose hand it is,
% so every passage in the batch is \hand{unidentified} and the note on leaf 3
% says why.
%
% The colour of the ink carries information and changes twice. On leaves 3 to 6
% the text is black and the money figures are in red; from leaf 7 the money
% figures go back to black and the red is transferred to the receipt line, so
% that from May 1914 « Received by check » and its figure are written in red
% across the whole width of the leaf. Leaf 10 keeps a payment date in pencil in
% the left margin, and leaf 3 an addition in pencil at the head of the money
% column.
%
% This is the part of Hopper's working life the paintings books do not record
% at all, and it is bookkeeping rather than art: two line drawings a week for
% the Associated Sunday Magazines at twenty dollars each, poster drawings for
% the U.S. Printing & Litho Co. against the Éclair film company's releases,
% halftones for A. W. Shaw's business magazines, an Eno's Fruit Salt
% advertisement for J. Walter Thompson, and - as the leaves reach November 1914
% - four war drawings for Everybodys' Magazine at twenty-five and forty
% dollars. The titles are the magazines' and not his: « An Obstreperous
% Roommate », « What a Smiles Worth in Wall St », « Mende Beilis under Arrest ».
%
% The arithmetic reconciles almost everywhere, which is worth saying because it
% is what makes the doubtful figures readable: the bill of $90 rendered on
% 19 March is the sum of the six unpaid litho entries above it, and the $80
% rendered on 26 May is the sum of the six after that. Where it does not
% reconcile - the pencil drawing entered at 12 on 4 March and billed at 10 two
% days later - the figures stand as written.

meter mark at the top right prints « LYNBROOK / JUL -2'63 / N.Y. » in a circle,
« U.S. POSTAGE » over « 05 » in a panel, and « PB METER » over « P.B 303848 »
below the eagle. The envelope is therefore fifty years later than the entries
the book opens with.}

\note{the four columns are the stationer's, not the writer's: the page comes
ruled with a red vertical rule at the left for the date and two more at the
right dividing dollars from cents, and no heading is written above any of them
anywhere in this batch. The columns are therefore left unheaded here. A figure
standing in the body of the leaf rather than in the money column --- the « 40 »
below « My First Order », the « 40 » on the last line --- is a running subtotal
carried from the entries above it. All the money figures on this leaf are in
red, the text in black.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch and is not either of the two
recorded from Books I to III: a fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters
and long descenders, quite unlike the small upright running hand of the later
volumes and unlike the drawn title lettering. The first entry is of November
1913. Nothing on these sheets identifies the writer, and no signed comparison
has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one.}
