% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 3: sheets 25 to 36, which are leaves 24 to 35, and carry
% sixteen months of illustration income from 10 May 1917 to 13 September 1918.
% Every sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no
% insertions, no blanks and no clippings.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The book has not changed shape since batches 1 and 2 and neither have its
% columns. The leaf comes ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for
% the date, two more at the right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading
% is written above any of them anywhere in the volume. The four columns are
% therefore left unheaded here, as they were in batches 1 and 2. The cents
% column is written far less often than in batch 2: most of this batch's
% figures are round dollars entered with the cents cell left blank, and those
% cells are blank on the leaf.
%
% The hand is the one hand of batches 1 and 2, unchanged: a fluent sloping
% copperplate, neither the small upright running hand nor the drawn title
% lettering of Books I to III. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the
% writer, and no signed comparison has been made, so every passage is
% \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The colour convention set on leaf 7 holds without exception: the entries are
% in black and every receipt line - its date, its words and its figure - is in
% red. Where a bill is rendered rather than paid, the line stays black. Two
% marks in the batch are in pencil and neither is an entry: the « 19 18 »
% above the date of 3 January on leaf 28, and nothing else.
%
% The clients turn over again, and the turn is the content of the batch. The
% Webb Publishing Co. and The Country Gentleman, who carried batch 2, stop
% after leaf 30; Every Week appears four times and stops; Adventure Magazine
% places two more of its long lists - fourteen drawings in July 1917 and twelve
% in March 1918. What replaces them is war work and a house organ. From
% January 1918 the Morse Dry Dock \& Repair Co. buys a two-colour cover for the
% Dry Dock Dial nearly every month, at 35 apiece, and Wells Fargo \& Co. buys
% War Savings Stamp covers at the same price; from June 1918 the agency
% Redfield-Kendrick-Odell buys rough colour sketches and dummy covers at 5 and
% 10 for war charities - Armenian relief, Gingling College, the Songs of the
% Soldiers - and the batch closes on a single poster, « Smash the Hun » for the
% U. S. Shipping Board, receipted at 300, which is ten times the largest figure
% anywhere else in the volume.
%
% The arithmetic reconciles wherever it can be tested, and settles several
% figures that the ink alone would not. Fourteen Adventure titles, thirteen at
% 15 and one at 30, make the 225 ruled off on leaf 25 and receipted on 8
% August; twelve at 15 make the 180 of leaf 31. The 70 receipted on 7 February
% is leaf 28's bill of 35 plus leaf 29's, and the leaf writes the sum in small
% figures under the second 35; the 70 of 25 May is the same trick on leaves 31
% and 32, and the 70 of 27 June the same again on leaf 33, where the first of
% the two bills is marked « forward » in red. The 40 receipted on 13 September
% is the whole of leaf 34's unpaid remainder once the overpayment of 20 on 20
% July is allowed for. Where the arithmetic and the ink disagree the ink
% stands: the 15 billed on leaf 26 for a Messenger drawing is the same figure
% as the 15 receipted against it, not a 16.

\note{the four columns are the stationer's and not the writer's, as they are
throughout the volume: the leaf comes ruled with a red vertical rule at the
left for the date and two more at the right dividing dollars from cents, and
no heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are
therefore left unheaded here. The entries are in black and every receipt line
--- date, words and figure --- is in red.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch, and is the hand of batches 1 and 2:
a fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters and long descenders, unlike
either the small upright running hand or the drawn title lettering recorded
from Books I to III. Nothing on these sheets identifies the writer and no
signed comparison has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one.}

\note{the last word of the Webb title is doubtful twice over. « Mother » ends
in the small open loop this hand uses for a terminal « s », so « Mothers »
without an apostrophe is the likeliest reading of it; the word after it opens
with a capital that will support « T » as readily as « G », and only the sense
of the line decides between « Town » and « Gown ». Neither the entry nor the
receipt of 20 settles it.}

\note{two receipt days on this leaf cannot be read. The 6 of « July 6 » is
written over an earlier figure and stands as one blot with a second stroke
through it; the day of the last receipt is a blot altogether, with only its
superscript « th » surviving above it. The two Country Gentleman entries are
dated 23 and 28 June and their receipts 29 June and July, so the second entry
stands below a receipt that predates it.}
