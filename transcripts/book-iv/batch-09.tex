% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 9: sheets 97 to 108, which are leaves 96 to 107, and carry just
% under eight years of income from 28 March 1930 to 1 January 1938. Every sheet
% in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions, no blanks
% and no clippings.
%
% Eight things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns have not changed and are still unheaded, as they have been since
% leaf 7, and the hand is the one hand of the eight earlier batches. Nothing on
% these twelve sheets identifies the writer, so every passage is
% \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The batch covers nearly three times the ground of any before it. Batch 8 spent
% twelve leaves on thirty-two months; these twelve carry ninety-three, because
% the Depression thins the book. A year now runs to about two leaves and rarely
% more: 1934 needs six entries on leaf 102 after four on leaf 101, where 1929
% had taken four leaves to itself.
%
% The discounts come back, and in a fourth notation. Batch 7 wrote « @ 33 1/3 \%
% » and batch 8 wrote nothing at all; this batch writes the deduction as a named
% cash sum in the description column - « less \$49.93 », « less photo \$16. »,
% « less photos \$29. », « less 18.38 photos », « less .50 for mats », « less
% commission and expenses ». Two of them can be checked and both close exactly:
% leaf 98's 4150.07 plus 49.93 is 4200, and leaf 103's 1181.62 plus 18.38 is
% 1200. It is the best arithmetic in the volume, and it is the photographer's
% bill being passed on rather than the dealer's rate being stated.
%
% Eleven of the twelve leaves carry a sum, and nine of those close to the cent.
% Leaf 104 closes its 1935 sub-sum exactly and its foot sum is rubbed past
% reading; leaf 103 refuses outright. Three year lines fall inside leaves - leaf
% 97 (1931), leaf 99 (1932), leaf 107 (1938) - and all three behave, each closing
% the year above it and opening a fresh sum below. Leaf 106 does the same for
% 1937 and leaf 104 for 1936.
%
% Leaf 100 carries no sum at all. It is the first leaf since the sums began at
% leaf 66 to run to the bottom edge without one, and 1933 is the loser: its two
% leaves, 100 and 101, are never totalled, because leaf 101's foot sum of
% 2423.34 answers only the part of it below the « 1934 » line. The 76.66 that
% 1933 earns above that line is summed nowhere.
%
% Leaf 103 is short by exactly one dollar, for the third time in the volume. Its
% four receipts make 1785.46 and its foot sum reads an unambiguous 1784.46 -
% the same slip in the dollars and not the cents that leaf 83 made at the end of
% batch 7 and leaf 86 made in batch 8. No figure has been adjusted to close it.
%
% Leaf 107 disagrees with itself the other way about, and it is the reverse of
% the shape batch 8 found. The Rehn entry of 1 January 1938 lists 1875, 900, 30
% and 80, which make 2885, and rules off a subtotal of 2835; the cheque of 1867
% is 2835 less the 968 commission, so the subtotal and the cheque agree against
% the items. On leaf 90 in batch 8 the leaf sum sided with the items against the
% cheque. Here it is the items that stand alone. Both figures stand as written.
%
% The buyers thin out and then change kind. Rehn takes almost everything -
% 3036.74, 4103.17, 4150.07, 2317.34, 2004.34, 1613.34, 2280.93 - and Weyhe,
% Keppel, Kennedy and the Downtown Gallery keep buying prints at a flat 20. But
% from 1934 the money starts coming from institutions rather than dealers: the
% Baltimore Museum's award for Night Windows, the Whitney Museum twice, the
% Dominion of Canada renting Chop Suey by the quarter for the United States
% exhibition at Ottawa, the Worcester purchase prize, the Corcoran's W. A. Clark
% first prize of 2000. Two lines are not income at all - « Pittsburgh travel
% expenses 50 » and « Springfield Travel expenses 14.76 » on leaf 104, each
% with a figure and no receipt.
