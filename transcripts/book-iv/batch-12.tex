% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 12: sheets 133 to 144, which are leaves 132 to 143, and carry
% four years and seven months from 17 May 1954 to 2 December 1958. Every sheet
% in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions and no
% blanks. One leaf carries paper that is not the leaf: a strip of gummed paper
% over the date column of leaf 132, and a slip of white paper laid over the
% money column at the foot of leaf 137 and written on.
%
% Nine things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns are still the stationer's and still unheaded, as they have been
% since leaf 7, and the hand of the entries is the one hand of the eleven
% earlier batches. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the writer, so the
% batch is attributed to no one.
%
% The colour rule acquires a fourth colour. Charges are black, receipts red and
% sums pencil, as they have been since leaf 7; on leaves 133 and 134 a blue
% pencil enters and does one thing only - it follows two cheques that were
% given back. « returned to John Clancy » stands across the net of the entries
% of 16 September and 2 October 1954, « Dec. 15 » and « Dec 20 » are added in
% blue to the red receipt lines of those same entries, and the first two
% entries of 1955 are written in blue throughout: « Rec'd by check from John
% Clancy Special », 3666.67 on 3 January and 3666.67 on 4 January, the same two
% figures over again. A pencil line beneath them says why - « delayed receipt
% Re Zucker --- p income Tax ». Seven thousand dollars of 1954 was moved into
% 1955, and the book records the move rather than covering it over.
%
% The buyers are named for the first time in Book IV. Leaf 133 carries three
% names in small pencil down its left margin, one against each picture: Roy
% Neuberger against the Barber Shop, Harold Harris against Martha McKeany
% Wellfleet, and Jo Hirshhorn against Hotel by a Railroad, Gloucester Street,
% First Row Orchestra and Eleven A.M. The pencil is smaller than the entries
% and the names appear on no other leaf of the batch. They are given as
% \marginal{} and attributed to no one.
%
% « Less commission » naming the net rather than the deduction is now the
% commoner reading of the two. Leaf 132's « less commission 3433.00 » stands
% against a subtotal of 5150, and 3433 is two thirds of it, not one; a payment
% on account of 2000 comes off below and the cheque is 1433.00. Leaf 134 and
% leaf 139 do it three times over on a thirty-dollar print, « less commission
% 1/3 » followed by 20.00 and a cheque for 20.00, where the commission is ten.
% Where the words do mean the deduction it is a flat third - 2500 of 7500,
% 1833.33 of 5500, 5500 of 16500, 2000 of 6000.
%
% Four sums do not follow from the figures above them, and one item does not
% follow from its own leaf. Leaf 137's first block prices three watercolours at
% 1850, 500 and 500 and rules them off at 2350; every figure after that -
% 783.32 of commission, 1566.68, 43.00 of frames, 1523.68 - is taken from the
% 2350, and the slip of paper at the foot receipts the chain, so the leaf sides
% with its subtotal against its items as leaf 131 did. Its second block prices
% 760, 400 and 675 and rules them off at 1825, ten short. That block's « less
% commission » line then reads 675.00, which is the price of The Forked Road
% written a second time rather than the third of 1825 the net below it uses:
% 1216.68 is right for 1825 and 675.00 answers to nothing. Leaf 136's net reads
% 6366.67 where 8050 less 2683.33 makes 5366.67, a thousand too high, and the
% « total » of 5333.44 written two lines below it is correct and is the figure
% the cheque paid.
%
% The leaf sums and the year sums disagree with each other about what to count.
% Leaf 134 rules off at 11333.34, which is exactly the two Clancy cheques and
% the Rehn 4000 and takes in neither the Artex royalty of 2.20 nor the Kennedy
% 20.00 standing on the same leaf; the year total on leaf 135 takes in both.
% Leaf 140's foot carries 24498.43 for 1957, which is that leaf and the 600 of
% leaf 139 with the July cheque counted as sixteen thousand rather than the
% 16,514.65 the next leaf writes - 514.65 short. Leaf 143's « Total » of
% 17897.37 is thirty-six dollars over its own two figures, 17737.84 and 123.53.
% The batch's other sums close: 12333.34 and 8147.14 and 123.53 are exact.
%
% Two entries were drafted in pencil and rubbed out. The July 12 entry of leaf
% 132 stands a second time below itself, the date and the client and a figure
% of 1433.00 still legible under the erasure; leaf 135 has a rubbed-out line
% naming Frank K. M. Rehn immediately above « Total 1955 ». Both are given as
% \note{} rather than transcribed, since what survives of them is the shape of
% the entry that was written again in ink above.
%
% A cheque is received in one year and banked in the next, and the book says
% so. Leaf 136 opens 1956 with two oils settled at 6000.00, receipts them
% « Dec 27 rec'd by check » with the year « 1955 » struck at the head of the
% line, and adds « Jan 3 deposited check » on the line below in the same red.
%
% The last entry of the batch is money going the other way. Below the 1958
% total, in pencil and unreceipted, leaf 143 carries « Dec 2 Cape & Vineyard
% Electric Co for electric line at Truro 914.00 ». Nothing else in these twelve
% leaves is a payment made rather than a payment received, and the entry above
% it was struck out and written again.

\note{the four columns are the stationer's and not the writer's, as they are

no heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are
therefore left unheaded here, in every leaf of the batch.}

batches 1 to 11. Nothing on these sheets identifies the writer and no signed
comparison has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one. Charges are
black, receipts red and sums pencil, as they have been since leaf 7; the blue
pencil that enters on the next two leaves is discussed there.}

the leaf across the last four lines of the July entry. It covers the date
column of the receipt line, which is why that date is \ill{}, and the first
letters of « payment on acct. » show past its edge. Red writing is visible
through it and cannot be read.}

the word beneath. It is four or five letters and is not read.}

\note{the subtotal is 5150.00 and its four items make 5150 exactly. « less
commission 3433.00 » is then the net and not the deduction --- two thirds of
5150 is 3433.33, and the payment on account of 2000 is taken off it to reach
the 1433.00 the cheque pays. The cents are dropped in the writing.}

\note{« payment on acct. » carries a minus sign written before the figure, in
the money column. It is the only line in the batch that does.}

\note{the whole July entry stands a second time below itself in pencil, rubbed
out: the date, the client's name and the last figure, 1433.00, can still be
made out under the erasure. It is not transcribed, being the draft of the
entry written in ink above it.}
