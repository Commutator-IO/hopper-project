% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 7: sheets 73 to 84, which are leaves 72 to 83, and carry two
% years and a half of income from 13 December 1924 to 18 May 1927. Every sheet
% in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions, no blanks
% and no clippings.
%
% Seven things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns have not changed and are still unheaded, as they have been since
% leaf 7. The leaf comes ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for the
% date, two more at the right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is
% written above any of them anywhere in the volume, so the four columns are left
% unheaded here as they were in batches 1 to 6. The hand is the one hand of the
% six earlier batches, unchanged, and nothing on these twelve sheets identifies
% the writer, so every passage is \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The illustration income stops. Hotel Management, the Ahrens Publishing Co
% cover that ran monthly through batch 6, is billed for the last time in
% November 1925 on leaf 76 and never again; Charles Scribner's Sons pays 175
% twice, in March and September 1925, and then stops. From leaf 77 the volume is
% a record of pictures and prints and of nothing else, which is the largest
% change in Book IV since it opened in 1913.
%
% What replaces it is Frank K. M. Rehn. He takes three water colours in December
% 1924, six in January 1925, three in December, three in October 1926, four in
% March 1927 and then, on 23 April 1927, one oil and eight water colours for
% 1924.04 after 209.30 of expenses - the largest figure in Book IV and three
% times the volume's previous best. His water colours are numbered in one
% unbroken series down the description column, at 100 apiece: leaf 71 in batch 6
% carried 1 to 5, leaf 72 carries 6 to 8 and leaf 73 carries 9 to 14. The series
% then stops, and the later Rehn entries name the pictures instead.
%
% The museums arrive. On 27 May 1925 the Metropolitan Museum takes fifteen
% prints at ten dollars each - one impression of nearly every plate Hopper had
% made - and the leaf lists all fifteen titles. In April 1926 the Art Institute
% of Chicago buys New York Restaurant for 450 through Mrs Coburn; in February
% 1926 Duncan Phillips buys St Francis Cathedral and in November 1926 the oil
% « Sunday »; in July 1926 E. P. Jennings sells etchings to the British Museum
% for one pound, receipted in cash and converted in pencil to five dollars
% beside it. That is the volume's only sterling figure.
%
% The leaf sums continue, and three of the twelve do not come out. The pencil
% sum under a pencil rule that began on leaf 66 in batch 6 runs through every
% leaf of this batch, and where a year line falls inside a leaf the leaf carries
% two sums, one closing the old year above the line and one closing the new
% below it. Nine reconcile to the cent. Leaf 76's receipts make 514.67 against a
% pencil 474.67; leaf 80's make 886.35 against a pencil figure whose second
% digit is a 6 in the shape of her pencil sixes; leaf 83's make 2217.71 against
% a pencil 2218.71. No figure has been adjusted to close any of the three.
%
% The prints get dearer and the discount gets simpler. Batch 6 found East Side
% Interior and Night Shadows at a list price of 18 and Evening Wind at 22, with
% the trade taking 10, 15, 20, 30 or 50 per cent off. In this batch the list is
% 25 for all three and the dealers take either a half or a third: Kennedy \& Co.
% at 50\%, Fred Keppel and Edith Halpert at 33 1/3 \%, and Rehn at 33 1/3 \% for
% pictures as well as prints. The commission is written into the description
% with an @ sign, which is new.
%
% Two running pencil tallies stand in the head margins, above the ruling and
% outside the entries: « 62 Etchings » at the head of leaf 78 and « 75 Etchings »
% at the head of leaf 79. Leaf 75 carries a small pencil addition in the same
% margin - 49 over 15 under a rule, then 64 - which answers to nothing on the
% leaf.

heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are therefore
left unheaded here. The entries are in black and every receipt line --- date,
words and figure --- is in red.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch, and is the hand of batches 1 to 6: a
fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters and long descenders. Nothing on
these sheets identifies the writer and no signed comparison has been made, so
the batch is attributed to no one.}

column, which continues the numbering of leaf 71 in batch 6, where five water
colours delivered to Rehn on 24 November 1924 were numbered 1 to 5. The series
runs on again on leaf 73 at 9. None of the pictures is named on any of the three
leaves, and the 100 against each is the net rather than the price.}

\note{« Dec » stands in red in the date column against the third of the three
water colours, one line above the receipt, and is struck through with a
horizontal stroke; the receipt line below it repeats « Dec 13 » in full. It is a
receipt line begun on the wrong row.}

\note{the leaf carries two pencil sums under two pencil rules. The first, 300,
stands between the Rehn receipt and the year line and closes 1924; the second,
117.00, stands at the foot and is the leaf's three 1925 receipts --- 9, 43 and
65 --- added exactly. The « 1925 » between them is written in pencil and has a
pencil oval drawn round it.}

\note{the initial in « Chas. J. Lane » is a loop rising to a peak at the right
with a straight descender carrying no lower loop. « Jan » on the same leaf gives
her J with a full looped descender, so the two are not the same stroke; G fits
the letter about as well as J.}

\note{the day of the February receipt is written over an earlier figure and
retraced until the strokes make a mass. 27 is what the surviving strokes carry
and the reading is doubted on the second digit.}
