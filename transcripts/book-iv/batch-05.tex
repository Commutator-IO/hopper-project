% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 5: sheets 49 to 60, which are leaves 48 to 59, and carry two
% and a half years of income from 22 April 1920 to 17 November 1922. Every
% sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions, no
% blanks and no clippings.
%
% Six things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns have not changed and are still unheaded. The leaf comes ruled by
% the stationer - a red rule at the left for the date, two more at the right
% dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is written above any of them
% anywhere in the volume, so the four columns are left unheaded here as they
% were in batches 1 to 4. What has changed is that the cents column is finally
% used. Batch 4 recorded it as unused throughout; on these leaves three entries
% fill it - 16.20 and 19.80 on leaves 49 and 54, both of them print sales - and
% every other figure in the batch is still a round dollar with the cents cell
% left blank on the leaf.
%
% The hand is the one hand of batches 1 to 4, unchanged: a fluent sloping
% copperplate. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the writer, and no
% signed comparison has been made, so every passage is \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The colour convention holds - entries in black, the receipt line and its date
% and figure in red - with the pencil exceptions batch 4 first met now common
% rather than rare. Four entries are wholly in pencil (the Morse cover of 3
% September on leaf 50, both Photoplay entries on leaf 53, the Everybodys'
% serial on leaf 57), always with the receipt still in red beneath them. On leaf
% 53 several black figures have been gone over in a second, bluish ink - the
% « 30 » of the Redfield bill and the last digit of the year line among them -
% and that retracing is what makes the year line readable at all.
%
% The volume starts selling prints, and this is the batch's real event. Six
% print sales stand among the illustration jobs, the first etching income Book
% IV has ever recorded: the Print Makers of Los Angeles take The Monhegan Boat
% at 18 less 10\%, Carl Smalley takes Somewhere in France at 12 less 33 1/2\%,
% the National Academy of Design takes The Bull Fight at 22 less 10\%, the
% Weyhe Book Shop takes three prints at 45 less 40\%, the Whitney Studio Club
% takes Night in the Park at a flat 20, and Brown Robertson Co takes four prints
% at 69 less 33 1/3\%. Book IV has no commission column - it never needed one
% for illustration - so the dealer's discount is written into the description
% instead, and what lands in the money column is the net. Each of the six is
% receipted by cheque to the cent, and none of them is billed: a print sale in
% this book has no « bill » line at all, only the terms and the cheque.
%
% The clients turn over a fourth time. The Morse Dry Dock Dial cover runs the
% whole batch and its price rises again - 45 in April 1920, 50 from May - with
% the subject rather than the month now written at the end of the description
% (painter, man at lathe, welder, boiler maker, carpenter, Cup Defender, Statue
% of Liberty). The Greeley Square Hotel Co keeps buying Tavern Topics covers and
% starts attaching terms to them, « 10\% discount 15 days », which is taken once
% - 60 billed on 21 June 1920 and 54 received on 3 July - and let lapse every
% other time. What is new is agency work: J. Walter Thompson, Calkins \& Holden,
% Barton Durstine \& Osborn, Redfield Kendrick Odell and The Blackman Co all
% appear for the first time, several of them against order numbers. And two
% magazines take long runs - Photoplay's « Vamps of All Times » at 35 a drawing
% across three leaves, and Everybodys' « Sacrifice » at 200 a part for five
% parts, which are the largest regular sums the volume has carried.
%
% The arithmetic reconciles better than in any earlier batch and settles several
% figures the ink alone would not. Four Vamps at 35 make the 140 ruled off on
% leaf 54, which is the only ruled-off sum in the batch. All six discounts come
% out exactly: 45 less 40\% is the 27 written, 69 less a third is 46, 22 less
% 10\% is 19.80, 18 less 10\% is 16.20. Two shapes refuse, and both stand as
% written: on leaf 50 a pencil bill of 60 is answered by a red cheque of 50, and
% on leaf 53 a bill read as 65 is answered by a cheque of 60. Neither is
% confirmed by anything else on its leaf, and neither has been adjusted.
%
% One thing on leaf 53 needs saying to whoever reads the accounts. The year is
% stated once on a line of its own, as it is throughout Book IV, and the line
% says « 1920 ». The entries under it run 1 January to 4 March and follow a
% December entry receipted on 7 February, so they are 1921 and the line is
% wrong. It has been transcribed as written.

\note{the four columns are the stationer's and not the writer's, as they are
throughout the volume: the leaf comes ruled with a red vertical rule at the
left for the date and two more at the right dividing dollars from cents, and no
heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are therefore
left unheaded here. The entries are in black and every receipt line --- date,
words and figure --- is in red.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch, and is the hand of batches 1 to 4:
a fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters and long descenders, unlike
either the small upright running hand or the drawn title lettering recorded
from Books I to III. Nothing on these sheets identifies the writer and no
signed comparison has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one.}

\note{the Greeley Square bill of 60 rendered on 29 April is the one charge in
this batch that nothing answers. No receipt follows it here, and the next
Greeley Square entry, on leaf 49, is a fresh order for the August cover.}

\note{a short slanted mark stands after the last figure, clear of the 5 and
above the line. It is not the low round stop this hand puts after « Los
Angeles. » and « Lilith. », and nothing else on the leaf carries one, so the
figure is entered as 75 and the mark recorded here rather than read as a point.}
