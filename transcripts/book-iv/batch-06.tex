% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 6: sheets 61 to 72, which are leaves 60 to 71, and carry two
% years and a quarter of income from 29 November 1922 to 27 January 1925. Every
% sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions, no
% blanks and no clippings.
%
% Seven things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns have not changed and are still unheaded, as they have been since
% leaf 7. The leaf comes ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for the
% date, two more at the right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is
% written above any of them anywhere in the volume, so the four columns are left
% unheaded here as they were in batches 1 to 5. The cents column, first used in
% batch 5 and only by print sales, is now in regular use: nine entries fill it,
% and they are still all print sales bar one.
%
% The hand is the one hand of batches 1 to 5, unchanged: a fluent sloping
% copperplate. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the writer, and no
% signed comparison has been made, so every passage is \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The volume starts adding up. From leaf 66 - the leaf on which the year line
% « 1924 » is written - each leaf carries a sum at its foot, in pencil, under a
% pencil rule drawn across the money columns: 12.50, 410.10, 256.50, 279, 317,
% 665. Nothing in Book IV has done this before. The first five are the leaf's
% own receipts added up and are exact to the cent, which makes them the best
% figure-check the volume has yet offered; the sixth is not, and that is the
% batch's one unreconciled shape. On leaf 66 the sum is 12.50 where the leaf's
% receipts come to 205.50, because the four entries above it are 1923 and only
% the Randolph cheque of 14 January falls under the new year line. The running
% therefore begins with 1924 rather than with the leaf.
%
% Leaf 71 does not add up, and it stands as written. Its four receipts are 500,
% 100, 100 and 65, which make 765; the pencil sum at the foot reads 665. The
% two hundreds are a plate and an advance royalty, separately charged and
% separately receipted, and neither is struck. Nothing on the leaf accounts for
% the difference, and no figure has been adjusted to close it.
%
% The colour rule holds - entries in black, the receipt line and its date and
% figure in red - with the exceptions batch 4 first met now settled into a
% habit. Twice, on leaves 65 and 67, a charge line carries its date in red with
% the figure beside it in black and no word at all in the description column,
% which is exactly the shape leaf 46 showed in batch 4. Pencil runs to whole
% entries again: the Geo Dyer job of 7 June on leaf 63, the Hotel Management
% dummy of 22 May on leaf 68 - which is billed to nobody and paid by nobody -
% and every one of the six leaf sums.
%
% The etchings become an income rather than an event. Batch 5 found the first
% six print sales in the volume's history; this batch carries twenty-two prints
% across fifteen sales, and East Side Interior alone is sold nine times. The
% shape batch 5 recorded holds throughout - no « bill » line, no date until the
% receipt, the dealer's percentage written into the description and the net in
% the money column - and the discounts still come out exactly: 90 less a half is
% 45, 36 less a fifth is 28.80, 18 less a tenth is 16.20, 102 less 15\% is
% 86.70. The buyers are the print trade and the societies: Sidney Phillips four
% times, the Chicago Society of Etchers (which awards the Logan prize in
% February 1923 and buys the plate that won it), the International Print Makers
% of Los Angeles (the Bryan prize, two months later), the Brooklyn Society of
% Etchers, Frederick Keppel, Kennedy \& Co., the Weyhe Book Shop, the Print
% Rooms, the State Library of California, the National Academy and the Randolph
% Collection. Mrs Albert Sterner buys on three separate occasions.
%
% Two entries are of another order altogether, and they are the reason this
% batch matters. On leaf 66, under 16 November 1923, the Brooklyn Museum takes
% The Mansarde Roof for 100 - the volume's first sale of a painting to a museum,
% written in one line between an advertising job and a Printers Ink drawing.
% And on leaf 71, under 24 November 1924, Frank K. M. Rehn takes five water
% colours at 33 1/3 \%, numbered 1 to 5 down the money column at 100 apiece, and
% receipts 500 on the same day. It is the largest single figure in Book IV and
% four times the volume's previous best.
%
% The clients turn over a fifth time. The Morse Dry Dock Dial and the Greeley
% Square Tavern Topics covers, which carried batches 4 and 5, are gone - Tavern
% Topics appears once, on leaf 60, and never again. What replaces them is
% Printers Ink, which takes eight Pop Ball drawings at 50 across five leaves;
% Geo. L. Dyer Co., which buys line drawings for Remington Arms against order
% numbers; Charles Scribner's Sons, at a steady 175 for three drawings; and from
% June 1924 the Ahrens Publishing Co., whose Hotel Management cover runs monthly
% at 65 to the end of the batch. Two entries are neither illustration nor print:
% Mrs L. Whitins pays 105 for seven weeks of instruction and 60 for four more,
% at 15 a week, which is the only teaching income in six batches.

\note{the Rehn entry is written like no other in the volume. The five water
colours are not named and not described: the commission is stated once at the
head, « at 33 1/3 \% », and the works are numbered 1 to 5 down the description
column with 100 against each, which is the net rather than the price. The cheque
of the same day is 500, written in red with the two noughts run together. It is
the largest figure in Book IV.}

\note{the New Republic block is two charges and two cheques, and the second
cheque is dated the day before the first. « plate Night Shadows » is charged 100
to the magazine and « advance royalties » 100 to Robert Hallowell, whose name
stands on its own line between them; the receipt of the 26th names « New Rep. »
and that of the 25th repeats « rec'd by check » in ditto marks and names
« R. Hallowell ». Neither charge is struck.}

\note{the leaf's sum does not come out. The pencil figure under the pencil rule
reads 665, and the four receipts above it --- 500, 100, 100 and 65 --- make 765.
The 6 and the 6 are the same rounded loop and neither is a 7. Nothing on the
leaf accounts for the hundred, and no figure has been adjusted; the five sums
before this one, on leaves 66 to 70, are all exact.}

no year. The year line above it is the « 1924 » of leaf 66.}
