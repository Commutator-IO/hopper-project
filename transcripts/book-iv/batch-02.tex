% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 2: sheets 13 to 24, which are leaves 12 to 23, and carry
% twenty-seven months of illustration income from 23 February 1915 to 5 May
% 1917. Every sheet in the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no
% insertions, no blanks and no clippings.
%
% Five things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The book has not changed shape since batch 1 and neither have its columns.
% The leaf comes ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for the date,
% two more at the right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is written
% above any of them anywhere in the volume. The four columns are therefore left
% unheaded here, as they were in batch 1. Leaf 13 is the exception and is
% treated in a note on that leaf: its money area carries no printed rules at
% all, and the first five figures on it are written without cents.
%
% The hand is the one hand of batch 1, unchanged: a fluent sloping copperplate,
% neither the small upright running hand nor the drawn title lettering of Books
% I to III. Nothing on these twelve sheets identifies the writer, and no signed
% comparison has been made, so every passage is \hand{unidentified}.
%
% The colour convention set on leaf 7 holds without exception across the whole
% batch: the entries are in black and every receipt line - its date, its words
% and its figure - is in red. Where a bill is rendered rather than paid, the
% line stays black. Nothing in this batch is in pencil except the « 10 » above
% the date of 12 March on leaf 12.
%
% The clients turn over completely between 1915 and 1917, and the turn is the
% content of the batch. The Associated Sunday Magazines, who bought two line
% drawings a week through batch 1, appear four times here and then stop. In
% their place come the Webb Publishing Co. of St Paul, farm subjects for The
% Country Gentleman, two long lists for Adventure Magazine - eleven drawings in
% November 1915 and fifteen in April 1916, at fifteen dollars each - and from
% November 1916 a steady run of poster, car-card and cover work for Wells Fargo
% \& Co. and for their house paper the Wells Fargo Messenger, placed latterly
% through the H. K. McCann Co. The De Vinne Press, Todays Magazine and Every
% Week each appear once or twice.
%
% The arithmetic reconciles wherever it can be tested, which is what makes the
% doubtful figures readable. Eleven drawings at 15 make the bill of 165 rendered
% on 24 November 1915; fifteen at 15 make the 225 of 25 May 1916, and the
% fifteenth of them is on the next leaf. The running total that opens 65 on leaf
% 21 takes 15 on leaf 22 to make 80, and the 40 at the foot of leaf 22 takes 40
% on leaf 23 to make 80 again - two bills carried across a leaf turn without
% being restated.

signed comparison has been made, so the batch is attributed to no one.}

been written over and stands as a blot. The receipt of 8 March carries no cents
figure, and « Trails End » is given quotation marks at its first appearance and
none at its second.}
