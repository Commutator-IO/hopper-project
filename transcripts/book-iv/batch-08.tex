% Pass: Opus 5 (claude-opus-5), 2026-09-09 - first pass, unchecked against the
% sheets by a human.
%
% Book IV, batch 8: sheets 85 to 96, which are leaves 84 to 95, and carry very
% nearly three years of income from 19 May 1927 to 28 March 1930. Every sheet in
% the batch is a written leaf; there are no covers, no insertions, no blanks and
% no clippings.
%
% Eight things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% The columns have not changed and are still unheaded, as they have been since
% leaf 7. The leaf comes ruled by the stationer - a red rule at the left for the
% date, two more at the right dividing dollars from cents - and no heading is
% written above any of them anywhere in the volume, so the four columns are left
% unheaded here as they were in batches 1 to 7. The hand is the one hand of the
% seven earlier batches, unchanged, and nothing on these twelve sheets
% identifies the writer, so every passage is \hand{unidentified}.
%
% Nothing here is illustration. Batch 7 found the last Hotel Management cover in
% November 1925 and the last Scribners drawing that September; twelve leaves and
% thirty-two months later there is still no illustration income of any kind. The
% unit throughout is a work and a dealer, and the volume has become a record of
% picture and print sales with no other business in it.
%
% The discount notation disappears. Batch 7 found « 1 etching @ 33 1/3 \% »
% written into the description and the net entered in the money column. In this
% batch no percentage, no @ sign and no « less » appears anywhere: only the net
% is written. The thirds survive in the figures - 16.67 for a print, 166.67 for
% a water colour - and the arithmetic that batch 7 could check against a stated
% rate can no longer be checked at all. Leaf 88 is the one place a third is
% written out, and even there it is only implied by the figure.
%
% The leaf sums continue, and eleven of the twelve come out to the cent. That is
% the best run in the volume - batch 7 managed nine of twelve - and the sums
% settle three doubtful figures in this batch on their own. The exception is
% leaf 86, which is discussed below.
%
% Three year lines fall inside leaves, and two of the three behave as batch 7
% said they would. Leaf 90 sums 291.34 above the « 1929 » line and 125.34 below
% it; leaf 94 sums 1841.67 above « 1930 » and 67 below. Leaf 86 does not: its
% sub-sum of 16.67 closes 1927 above the « 1928 » line, but its foot sum of
% 1256.68 answers to neither year. The four receipts below the line make
% 1241.01, and the whole leaf makes 1257.68 - so the foot sum is the whole leaf
% short by exactly one dollar, which is the shape leaf 83 refused in the same
% way at the end of batch 7. No figure has been adjusted to close it.
%
% One cheque disagrees with the items it answers, and the leaf sum takes the
% items' side. Leaf 90's Halpert entry of 7 January 1929 charges 20, 16.67 and
% 16.67, which make 53.34, and receipts a red 53.84 whose second cent digit is an
% unambiguous double-bowled 8 at 500\%. The leaf's own foot sum of 125.34 is
% 53.34 plus 72 exactly. Both figures stand as written.
%
% The buyers multiply and the prices fall. Rehn still takes the largest sums -
% 1452.68, 2248.34, 1783.67, 3232.34, 3380.17, and two oils at 1666.67 apiece -
% and Fred Keppel \& Co. still buys prints by the dozen. But eight names new to
% the volume appear beside them: the Whitney Studio Club (which becomes plain
% « Whitney Studio » in 1929), Vickery Atkins \& Torrey, Kennedy \& Co, E \& A
% Milch, the Am. Federation of Arts, Babcock Galleries, the Downtown Gallery -
% and five private buyers who are named and nothing more. Meanwhile East Side
% Interior, which sells for 25 in May 1927, sells for 16.67 in December, 20 in
% January 1929, and a flat 20 to three different galleries in 1930.

\note{the four columns are the stationer's and not the writer's, as they are
throughout the volume: the leaf comes ruled with a red vertical rule at the left
for the date and two more at the right dividing dollars from cents, and no
heading is written above any of them anywhere in this book. They are therefore
left unheaded here. The entries are in black and every receipt line --- date,
words and figure --- is in red.}

\note{the hand is one throughout the batch, and is the hand of batches 1 to 7: a
fluent sloping copperplate with joined letters and long descenders. Nothing on
these sheets identifies the writer and no signed comparison has been made, so
the batch is attributed to no one.}

\note{the client of 25 May is « Geo », then a single glyph standing where an
initial would, then a surname. The glyph is a loop closed at the top with an
ascender curving left, and it is not the capital S this hand writes in « Mrs. S.
Tucker » on leaf 91 --- which is a tall slanted loop with a hook --- so it is
left unread. The surname is a tall loop, a second tall loop, and « ian », which
carries « Allian » and « Ullian » about equally.}

\note{the last two entries, the Rehn oil of 6 October and the whole Keppel
entry, are rubbed and pale; the client line of the Keppel entry and the words
« 8 prints » beneath it are the faintest writing on the leaf. Both figures are
legible and both are repeated in red on the receipt line below.}

\note{the pencil sum at the foot, 1083.01, is the leaf's five receipts ---
333.34, 25, 12, 600 and 112.67 --- added exactly.}
