% Book V, batch 1: sheets 1 to 12 - the front cover, two thumb-indexed leaves
% with no number written on them, and leaves 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 9, 11, 13 and 15.
% Book V holds sixteen sheets in all, so this batch is all of it but the three
% Rehn receipt leaves and the back cover, which are batch 2.
%
% Six things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% Book V is not shaped like Books I or II, and it says so on its own second
% leaf: « This book for drawings, not water colors. » There is no ruled table
% of exhibitions, no accepted / Refused column, no running total. A leaf holds
% two to four of Edward Hopper's ink record drawings down the left, each with a
% title and a measurement in pencil beneath it, and beside each drawing one
% line of Jo Hopper's: a buyer, a price, the third off for the dealer, a date.
% That is the whole form of the volume, and it is repeated nine times here.
%
% The odd-numbered foliation is the Whitney's, not a gap in the record. Leaves
% 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 are rectos with blank versos that were not
% photographed; leaf 6 is in the batch because somebody wrote on it. The two
% leaves before leaf 1 carry no number at all - they are the stationer's
% thumb-indexed A-Z pages, used here as an index and a page of loans.
%
% Leaf 6 is the one ruled table, and it heads none of its columns. The four
% supplied in \add{} - title, buyer, price, date - are supplied because the
% sense of each is not in doubt from the rows beneath, and this note is the
% record that they are ours and not hers.
%
% The prices are all of one kind and it is worth saying once: a drawing is
% priced at 100 to 500, a third goes to the gallery, and what she writes is the
% subtraction, « 175 - 1/3 = 116 2/3 ». Nothing is totalled anywhere in the
% batch. Two figures do not reconcile with the arithmetic around them and both
% are transcribed as they stand, with a note.
%
% Edward Hopper's hand appears in this batch only inside the drawings - « To
% Josie E. H. », « For Jo Edward Hopper », his signature. The titles and
% measurements in pencil beneath each drawing are a light cursive, not his
% drawn lettering, and are taken as hers on the strength of the letterforms.
% Where a leaf is dense with pencil that will not settle, the words that will
% not settle are marked and the rest stands.
%
% Leaves 9, 11 and 13 photograph heavy show-through from the leaves behind
% them: on the right-hand half of each sheet a grey ghost of another leaf's
% entries is legible enough to tempt a reader. It is not writing on these
% leaves and it is not transcribed. Each of the three carries a note saying so.
