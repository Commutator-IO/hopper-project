% READ THIS FIRST. This batch was transcribed in the same session as Book V
% batch 1, immediately after it, at the reader's explicit direction. That puts
% it past the twelve-sheet limit the method sets for one pass: sheets 13 to 15
% here are the thirteenth to fifteenth leaves read without a break. The limit
% exists because reading degrades towards the end of a pass with nothing to
% signal it, and a transcription whose weakening point is unknown cannot be
% used. The weakening point of this file, if it has one, is its beginning
% rather than its end - leaf 51 is by a long way the densest leaf in the volume
% and it was read thirteenth. A checking pass should start there.
%
% Book V, batch 2: sheets 13 to 16 - leaves 51, 52, 53 and the back cover. The
% back cover carries no writing of any kind, only the stationer's printed
% border, so it has no entry here; the gap in the sequence is the record.
%
% Four things worth knowing before reading it.
%
% These three leaves are a different document from the rest of Book V. The
% first fifteen leaves record drawings; these record etchings, and they are the
% « p. 51 » the index on the volume's second leaf points to. There is not one
% record drawing on any of them.
%
% Leaf 51 is four lists, not one. She rules no columns and heads none, but she
% writes four separate headings down the leaf and starts again under each:
% eleven etchings Frank Rehn took from the studio in May 1951, then what the
% gallery had before that date, then what came from the Kleeman Gallery in
% 1953, then a fourth group under a question she puts to herself. They are set
% here as four tables with a heading line above each, and the four column names
% are supplied and are ours.
%
% She counts eleven etchings and lists nine. That is on the leaf and it is not
% reconciled here. Two other figures resist reading: a fraction on the question
% line that reads four over three where every other fraction on the leaf is one
% third, and a struck and rewritten figure on leaf 52 whose column total the
% surrounding rows only reach if it is 30. Both stand as they are, with notes.
%
% The two receipts leaves are where the volume stops being a private record and
% starts arguing with itself. Leaf 52 copies a Rehn statement of July 1959 and
% then asks, in the margin beside it, where the gallery got the prints it sold;
% leaf 53 records the Philadelphia Museum's purchase of the whole etched work
% for \$3500 in February 1962, and the seven initial drawings for plates given
% to Carl Zigrosser with it. Leaf 53 is the last written leaf of the volume.
