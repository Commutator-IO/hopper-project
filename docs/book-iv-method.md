# How Book IV keeps its accounts, and how the reader follows it

Book IV is the one volume of the six that is a cash book: a stationer's pocket
memorandum book, ruled by the stationer into a date column, a description
column and a money column divided into dollars and cents, with nothing headed
by the writer anywhere in its 159 leaves. It runs without a gap from 15
November 1913 to 23 March 1967. This note is what fourteen batches of
transcription, their headers and their notes, and one pass of reading the ink
off the photographs (#19) have established about the writer's method — period
by period, because the method changes four times — and what
`scripts/accounts.mjs` does with each shape. It is a guess in the sense that
nobody has asked the writer; it is not a guess in the sense that every claim
below points at a leaf.

The figures the volume states for itself are the check on all of it: from
leaf 66 a pencil sum at the foot of nearly every leaf, and from leaf 114 a
labelled total for the year. Those sums are of **receipts**, never of charges,
and the reader's receipts stream reconciles with them to the cent wherever the
leaf's own arithmetic closes (batch 11: 1946, 1947, 1949, 1951; batch 13:
1961, 1962). The charge side has no such check in the book, which is why it is
the side that has read high.

## The four periods

### I. 1913–1924, illustration (leaves 3–71)

An entry is a client line — the date and the payer — then the items one to a
line, then the settlement. Leaves 3 to 6 write the **price of each item in
red** and the receipt in black; from leaf 7 the colours swap and hold for the
rest of the volume: charges and their figures black, the receipt line — its
date, « Rec'd by check », its figure — red. Where a bill is rendered before
payment there is a black « bill » or « bill rendered » line carrying the sum of
the items above it, and the red receipt answers the bill. Two bills can be
paid by one cheque across a leaf-turn (leaves 21–23, 28–29), and the leaf
sometimes writes the addition in small figures under the second bill.

Prints arrive in 1920 in a different shape: no bill, the dealer's discount
written into the description (« 18 less 10% », « 4 prints @ 69 less 33 1/3 % »)
and the **net** in the money column, receipted to the cent. From leaf 66
(1924) a pencil sum at the foot of each leaf adds up the leaf's receipts.

*Reader:* items are charges; a « bill » is a charge only where nothing was
itemised since the last settlement; « less N% » loose in a line is a
discounted print at the figure written, never a deduction.

### II. 1924–1937, pictures and prints through Rehn (leaves 71–107)

Illustration stops in November 1925 and the book becomes a record of pictures
and prints sold, nearly all through Frank K. M. Rehn. Batch 7 writes the
commission as « @ 33 1/3 % » in the description; batch 8 writes **no rate at
all** and enters only the net (16.67 for a 25-dollar print, 166.67 for a
250-dollar water colour); batch 9 writes the deductions as named cash sums —
« less $49.93 », « less photo $16. », « less .50 for mats » — which is the
photographer's bill passed on, not the dealer's rate. Water colours sold to
Rehn in 1924–25 are numbered 1 to 14 down the description column at 100
apiece. Year lines fall inside leaves from 1928 and the pencil sums close the
year above the line and open a fresh one below it.

*Reader:* the figure written is the charge, at whatever the leaf writes (net
where only the net is written); a « less » line is a deduction.

### III. 1938–1955, the gallery account (leaves 107–135)

One sale is written down the leaf four or five times over, and only the first
is the charge. The shape is: items at list price, one to a line, medium marked
(« o », « w.c », « E »); a **rule drawn across the money column and the sum
ruled off under it**; « less commission », which names **either the deduction
or the net** — the same words for both, and only the arithmetic tells them
apart (733.34 against 1100 is the net; 1016.66 against 3050 is the third);
« less photos » or « less photos and frame »; the net restated, bare or under
« total »; and the red « rec'd by check ». The commission is a flat third to
the dollar, twice a cent under. A payment already had is taken off the net
(« payment on acct. −2000 », leaf 132). A red receipt is often first written
across the row that already carries the black net, rubbed out, and written
again on the line below (nine leaves in batches 10–11).

The book states its own rule about what counts: on leaf 116, « not included
in income » with a pencil cross against a print sale; leaf 108's foot sum
takes the Rehn cheque and leaves out four travel refunds. Refunds, insurance
and — inconsistently — prize money are left out of the sums.

*Reader:* items are charges; a bare figure is a subtotal where a bill, a
« less » line or a carried page-total follows it, where a cheque follows and
the priced lines above add to it, or where the cheque repeats it to the cent
under a priced item; a « less » line is a deduction whichever thing it names;
what stands between it and the cheque is the net restated; a receipt standing
on the net's row and repeated below is the net.

### IV. 1955–1967, statements (leaves 136–159)

From 1955 the entry is the gallery's **statement**, headed « Rec'd from Rehn
Gallery » (a charge line, despite the word) with the statement's date, and
then one of two shapes. Either the items at gross with the buyers named
(Charles Stein, the Hacketts, Fleischman), a ruled sum, « less 33 1/3 % », the
net, expenses (photographs, frames, a relining), and the red cheque; or the
cheque first — « check No 6388 date Feb 14 / Empire Trust Co / 7798.43 » — with
the items counted but not priced (« 10 water colors / 4 drawings / 3 etchings
/ 3 oils 16,514.65 »), so that the only figure in the book is the net.

**A picture is now paid for in parts**, and each part is written as an entry
of its own: « Excursion into Philosophy / on acct. 5000 » among a statement's
items in January 1962, then the picture at 14500 in June with « less payment
on acount 3333.34 » — the 5000 less a third — taken off the net; « A Woman in
the Sun 10000 » in June 1962, then 15000 in January 1963 with « Payment on
Account / Hackett 6666.67 » deducted; « Road and Trees » at 15000 twice on one
leaf, 7500 received on account and 2500 as the balance; « Intermission » 6000
on account in December 1965, « balance on Intermission 25000 » in July 1966
with the 6000 deducted. Read row by row every one of those pictures was
charged twice, and 1962–63 read 25,000 high on their account.

Blue pencil follows cheques returned to John Clancy (leaves 133–134); a cheque
received in December and deposited in January is entered in both years and
the book says so (leaves 136, 158–159); from leaf 157 the red receipt is
sometimes the figure alone, repeated under the charge with no words at all.

*Reader:* items are charges; a « payment on account » under a commission
already taken is a deduction, at the head of a statement it introduces the
instalment, which is the charge; **a title charged more than once, where one
of its entries says on account or balance or the entry carrying the price
deducts a payment on account, is one picture charged once at the largest
figure**, the others collapsed and reported in `illustration.instalments`; a
cheque-first statement is charged at the net, which is all the book writes.

## What is still read high, and known

- A picture whose price is never written in full — « Chair Car », 10000 on
  account and a balance received of 8000 — is charged at its instalments.
- Money going the other way — leaf 143's « Cape & Vineyard Electric Co for
  electric line at Truro 914 », in pencil and unreceipted — is a charge.
- Leaf 89's foot sum of 3615.34, which counts an unreceipted fifteen dollars
  in with the cheques, and leaf 108's foot sum of 600 inheriting a « rec'd by
  check » written with no figure (#22).
- Where the ink is not yet recorded, the words decide, and the words go blind
  from leaf 157 (#19). Where it is recorded — twelve leaves so far — red
  money is a receipt and pencil money a sum before any word is read.

## What is still missing from the transcription

The ink pass compares each leaf's rows with the written lines of its
photograph, and on leaf 21 (batch 2) found a whole entry untranscribed — The
Country Gentleman, 26 February 1917, 35.00 and its receipt — with the second
entry's « bill rendered 30 00 » and receipt missing too. Seventy-four leaves
show at least one written line the transcription lacks, thirty of them red;
many are the alignment's own noise and each has to be looked at. That is the
re-reading pass, and `npm run ink -- book-iv --why` is its worklist.
